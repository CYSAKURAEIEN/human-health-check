## 🌱 Growing basics

### Soil

#### Soil types

**Sandy soil**:
- Features: well aerated, drains fast, poor in nutrients
- Improve with: more organic matter (compost, leaf mould)
- Suits: root crops, beans and peas

**Clay soil**:
- Features: holds water well, drains badly, heavy and sticky
- Improve with: sand and organic matter, deep digging
- Suits: rice, Chinese cabbage

**Loam** (the best):
- Features: a balanced mix of sand and clay, fertile
- Suits: most crops

#### Improving soil

```
Making compost:
1. Materials:
   - Green (nitrogen): vegetable leaves, grass, coffee grounds
   - Brown (carbon): dry leaves, sawdust, paper
   - Ratio: green 1 : brown 2-3
2. Method:
   - Build it up in layers
   - Keep it moist
   - Turn it regularly
   - A hot heap is ready in 2-3 months, a cold heap in six months to a year
3. Use:
   - Mix it into the soil
   - Spread it on the surface
```

### Water management

**Watering rules**:
```
✓ When: early morning or evening
✓ How: water the roots and avoid the leaves (prevents disease)
✓ How often: let the soil dry, then water thoroughly
✓ How deep: soak it through (encourages deep roots)
✓ Drainage: do not let water stand
```

**Signs of thirst**:
- Wilting leaves
- Dry soil
- Dull colour

### Sunlight

**Full sun** (6-8 hours):
- Fruiting vegetables: tomatoes, peppers
- Cucurbits: cucumbers, watermelons
- Root crops: carrots, potatoes

**Partial sun** (3-6 hours):
- Leafy vegetables: lettuce, spinach
- Brassicas: Chinese cabbage, broccoli

**Shade-tolerant** (2-3 hours):
- Ginger, taro
- Some herbs

### Fertilising

**The three main nutrients**:
- **N** (nitrogen): leaf growth
- **P** (phosphorus): roots, flowers, fruit
- **K** (potassium): overall health, disease resistance

**Organic fertilisers**:
- Compost
- Animal manure (well rotted)
- Bone meal (phosphorus)
- Wood ash (potassium)
- Green manure (legumes)

**Fertilising rules**:
```
✓ Little and often
✓ Keep it off the base of the stem
✓ Fertilise after watering
✓ Different needs at different stages:
  - Growing: more nitrogen
  - Flowering: more phosphorus
  - Fruiting: more potassium
```

## 🌾 Crop rotation

### Principles

Do not grow crops of the same family on the same plot year after year

⚠️ Potatoes belong to the same family (Solanaceae) as tomatoes, peppers and aubergines and share their diseases (such as late blight). Put them all in the same year; do not follow one with another

### Rotation plan (4 years)

```
Year 1: legumes (fix nitrogen) - peas, soya beans
Year 2: leafy vegetables - Chinese cabbage, lettuce
Year 3: Solanaceae - tomatoes, peppers, aubergines, potatoes
Year 4: root crops - radishes, carrots
```

### Benefits

```
✓ Fewer pests and diseases
✓ Balanced soil nutrients
✓ Higher yields
✓ Less chemical fertiliser
```

## 🐛 Pest and disease management

### Prevention

```
✓ Choose disease-resistant varieties
✓ Do not plant too densely (air must move)
✓ Remove diseased plants and fallen leaves
✓ Rotate crops
✓ Companion planting (pest-repelling plants)
```

### Organic control

**Physical methods**:
- Pick pests off by hand
- Yellow sticky traps
- Insect-proof netting
- Wash pests off with clean water

**Biological methods**:
- Bring in natural enemies (ladybirds eat aphids)
- Grow pest-repelling plants:
  * Marigold: repels nematodes
  * Mint: repels ants and aphids
  * Garlic: repels many pests

**Home-made insecticides**:
```
Chilli water:
- Dried chillies + water, bring to the boil
- Let it cool, then spray
- Repels pests

Soapy water:
- A little washing-up liquid + water
- Spray it directly on the insects
- Kills aphids and whitefly

Garlic water:
- Crush garlic and soak it in water
- Strain, then spray
- Broad-spectrum repellent
```

## 📅 Seasonal planting calendar (northern temperate zone)

### Spring (March-May)

**Sowing**:
- Early: peas, celtuce, spinach
- Mid: tomatoes and peppers (raise seedlings), carrots
- Late: beans, maize, cucumbers

### Summer (June-August)

**Sowing**:
- Vegetables for autumn harvest
- Chinese cabbage, radish, spinach

**Care**:
- Watering (not at midday)
- Weeding
- Pest and disease control

### Autumn (September-November)

**Sowing**:
- Garlic, onions
- Cold-hardy vegetables

**Harvest**:
- Summer crops
- Store for the winter

### Winter (December-February)

**Protection**:
- Cover to keep warm
- Grow under glass or plastic

**Planning**:
- Plan for next year
- Prepare seed

## 🌟 Self-sufficiency guide

### Basic set-up (family of four)

**Growing area**:
- Vegetables: 50-100 m² (roughly self-sufficient)
- Grain: a family of four needs about 3000-5000 m² (0.3-0.5 ha) of grain fields to be self-sufficient; see [Staple crops](../../../docs/04-动植物培育/02-粮食作物.md#自给自足配置1家4口)
- Fruit trees: a few

**Animals** (optional):
- Chickens: 5-10 (eggs, meat)
- Rabbits: a few (meat)

**What to expect**:
- Roughly self-sufficient in vegetables
- More eggs than you need
- Some meat
- It takes time and work

### Closed-loop system

```
Kitchen scraps → chickens → chicken manure → compost → vegetables → kitchen scraps
```

### Time commitment

```
Daily: 1-2 hours
Peak times (sowing, harvest): more
You get faster with experience
```
