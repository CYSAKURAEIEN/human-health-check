## Wasps (hornets)

In the Chinese countryside, **wasps injure people more often than snakes or bears**. In autumn (roughly August to October) wasp nests are at their largest and most aggressive, and every year in Shaanxi, Yunnan, Hubei and elsewhere people die after being stung by swarms.

Wasps are different from honeybees: a honeybee stings once, leaves its sting in the skin and dies; **a wasp can sting again and again, its venom is stronger, and wasps often attack in swarms**.

### Prevention

```
✓ Look out for nests in trees, bushes, rock crevices, under eaves and in holes in the
  ground, and give them a wide berth
✓ If the buzzing gets louder or you see many wasps going in and out of one place
  → stop and change route
✓ Wear light, plain-coloured clothing; do not use perfume, hairspray or other scented
  products
✓ Wear a hat
❌ Do not poke nests, throw stones at them or burn them
❌ Do not make loud noise or wave your arms about quickly near a nest
✓ If you find a wasp nest near where you live, call 119 or get professionals to remove
  it; do not deal with it yourself
```

### When attacked by a swarm

```
✓ Immediately cover your head and neck with clothing or a jacket
✓ Get away as fast as you can, running away from the nest, at least several tens of
  metres
✓ If you can get into a building or car, get in and shut the doors and windows
✓ If there is no time to run, crouch or lie down and wrap your head and neck in clothing
❌ Do not stand and swat at them (it brings more wasps)
❌ Do not jump into water (the wasps will wait above the surface)
```

### After being stung

```
1. Get away from the nest
2. If it was a honeybee and the sting is still in: scrape it out with the edge of a
   card; do not pinch it out with your fingers
3. Wash the sting with soap and water
4. Apply something cold to reduce swelling and pain
5. Remove rings and bracelets (fingers and wrists may swell)

⚠️ Get medical help at once (ideally within 6 hours of the stings) if:
- There are any signs of allergy: a rash all over, swollen lips or face, difficulty
  breathing, tight throat, dizziness, racing heart → this may be anaphylactic shock;
  call 120 at once
- There are many stings: more than 10, even if there is no discomfort for now
- Stings in the mouth, throat or near the eyes
- Children, elderly people, or people with heart disease or allergies

⚠️ In the days after multiple stings, get medical help at once if:
- The urine turns dark, like strong tea or soy sauce
- You pass much less urine
- Aching, weak muscles all over, nausea, vomiting
  (wasp venom destroys muscle and red blood cells and causes acute kidney failure; this
  is the main cause of death from swarm stings)
```

For general treatment of insect bites and stings, see [Wound and Injury Care – Insect bites and stings](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/02-wounds-and-injuries.md#insect-bites-and-stings); for anaphylactic shock, see [Acute Symptoms – Allergic reactions](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/05-acute-symptoms.md#allergic-reactions).

## Ticks

Ticks live in grass, bushes and woods. Once on a person, they attach to the skin and suck blood, and can keep feeding for several days. **The bite usually does not hurt or itch; many people only find the tick in the shower.**

Ticks spread many diseases; common ones in China include:
- **Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS)**: mainly in the hilly and mountainous areas of central and eastern China; the death rate is fairly high; the patient's blood and body fluids are also infectious
- **Tick-borne encephalitis (forest encephalitis)**: mainly in the forest areas of the north-east and north-west
- **Lyme disease**: a ring-shaped red rash that slowly spreads around the bite

### Prevention

```
✓ Before going into long grass or woods:
  - Wear long sleeves and long trousers, tuck trouser legs into socks and your top into
    your trousers
  - Wear light-coloured clothing (makes ticks crawling on you easier to spot)
  - Put insect repellent containing DEET on exposed skin, following the instructions
✓ Keep to the middle of the path where you can; do not sit or lie on grass, and do not
  spread clothes on the grass
✓ After leaving, check your whole body carefully, especially:
  scalp, behind the ears, neck, armpits, waist, navel, groin, backs of the knees
✓ Shake out your clothes and check your rucksack; wash clothes brought home in hot
  water and tumble-dry them on high heat
✓ Check pet dogs too if they have been in long grass
```

### Finding a tick attached to you

```
✓ Use fine-pointed tweezers; grip the tick by its head (mouthparts) as close to the
  skin as possible
✓ Pull straight out, perpendicular to the skin, slowly and steadily
✓ After removing it, disinfect the bite with povidone-iodine or alcohol and wash your hands
✓ Put the tick in a sealed bag or photograph it, and note the date of the bite
  (to show the doctor if you fall ill)

❌ Do not pinch or squeeze the tick's body with your fingers
❌ Do not burn it, touch it with a cigarette end, or smother it in oil or nail varnish
   to make it "back out"
❌ Do not yank it or twist it from side to side
⚠️ If you cannot get it out, or the mouthparts break off in the skin, go to hospital
```

### After a tick bite

```
⚠️ Within about 1 month of the bite, see a doctor as soon as possible, and tell them
   yourself that you were bitten by a tick, if you have:
- Fever, tiredness, headache, aching muscles
- Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, loss of appetite
- A spreading red rash around the bite
- Bleeding gums, pinpoint bleeding spots on the skin
- Worsening alertness, drowsiness, convulsions

✓ When caring for someone with SFTS, wear gloves when touching blood or body fluids,
  and wash your hands afterwards
✓ People who work for long periods in the forest areas of the north-east and north-west
  can ask about tick-borne encephalitis vaccination
```

## Land leeches

In the rainy season in the mountain forests and rainforests of the south, land leeches (mountain leeches) are often found on the ground and on leaves; they get into shoes, socks and trouser legs to suck blood.

```
Prevention:
✓ Wear leech socks, or tuck trouser legs into socks and tie them tight
✓ Put insect repellent on shoes, socks and trouser legs; soap or salt water also works
✓ Stop every so often to check your ankles, calves and waist

After a bite:
✓ Slowly push the leech's sucker off along the skin with a fingernail, or wait for it
  to fill up and drop off by itself
✓ You can also sprinkle salt on it or drip alcohol or medicated oil (such as
  *fengyoujing*, a menthol oil) on it to make it let go
✓ The bite will bleed for quite a long time (leech saliva stops blood clotting easily);
  press to stop the bleeding, disinfect and dress it
❌ Do not pull it off by force (mouthparts left in the skin easily get infected)
```

## Snakes

Most snakes in China are not venomous, and venomous snakes rarely attack people unprovoked. Most bites happen because someone steps on a snake, reaches out to grab it, or provokes it.

```
Prevention:
✓ Wear long trousers and high-topped boots; as you walk, beat the grass ahead with a
  trekking pole or stick ("beat the grass to startle the snake")
✓ Always light your way with a head torch or torch when walking at night (many
  venomous snakes are active at night)
✓ Do not put your hands into rock crevices, tree holes or piles of grass you cannot see
  into; do not turn over stones casually
✓ Clear the ground before pitching a tent; check your sleeping bag and shoes before
  sleeping and before putting shoes on
❌ Do not catch, play with or beat snakes
❌ A snake that has just been killed, or a severed snake head, can still bite

If you meet a snake:
✓ Stop, back away slowly and leave it a way to escape
✓ Most snakes will leave by themselves
```

For what to do after a snakebite (do not cut it, do not suck it, get medical help at once), see [Wound and Injury Care – Snakebite](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/02-wounds-and-injuries.md#snakebites).

## Wild boar, dogs, monkeys and others

### Wild boar

In recent years wild boar numbers have risen in many places, and they can be seen in mountain villages and on the edges of towns.

```
✓ If you see one in the distance, go round it; do not approach, especially a sow with
  piglets
✓ Do not chase, feed, photograph or tease them
✓ If a boar charges: get behind a big tree, or climb up a tree, a big rock or other
  high place
✓ With small children, pick them up
⚠️ See a doctor if bitten or gored by a wild boar (the wounds are deep and easily
   infected)
```

### Dogs

```
Guard dogs and stray dogs are common in villages and the mountains:
✓ Do not run (running makes them chase and bite)
✓ Face the dog and back away slowly; do not stare it in the eye
✓ Hold a trekking pole or rucksack in front of you
✓ If knocked down, curl into a ball and protect your head and neck with your hands
⚠️ If bitten or scratched by a dog, treat it as a rabies exposure and get medical help
   as soon as possible
```

### Monkeys

```
Monkeys in scenic areas such as Emeishan and Huangshan snatch food and scratch people:
✓ Do not feed them; put food and plastic bags away in your rucksack
✓ Do not stare at a monkey or bare your teeth at it (to a monkey this is a challenge)
✓ If a monkey comes close, put down what you are holding and walk away slowly
⚠️ If bitten or scratched by a monkey, treat it as a rabies exposure and get medical
   help as soon as possible
```

For treating animal bites and preventing rabies, see [Wound and Injury Care – Bites](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/02-wounds-and-injuries.md#bites); for bears and other large animals, see [Forest Survival – Animals](02-forest-survival.md#animals).

## 📝 Key points

```
1. Wasps are most dangerous in autumn: keep away from nests; if chased, protect your
   head and neck and run; do not jump into water
2. More than 10 stings, or any signs of allergy: get medical help at once; dark urine in
   the following days means watch for kidney failure
3. Tuck in your trouser legs before entering long grass, and check your whole body for
   ticks afterwards
4. Grip the tick's head with tweezers close to the skin and pull straight out; do not
   burn it or smother it in oil
5. Fever within about 1 month of a tick bite: see a doctor and tell them you were bitten
6. Snakes: beat the grass ahead, light your way at night, do not put your hands into
   crevices, do not catch snakes
7. Go round wild boar, do not run from dogs, do not feed monkeys
8. If bitten or scratched by a dog or monkey, treat it as a rabies exposure
```
