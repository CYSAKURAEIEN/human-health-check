# Long-term Collapse Survival Guide

⚠️ **This chapter gives long-term survival strategies for the extreme case where social order has seriously broken down**

```
⚠️ First, be clear what this chapter is for
- This chapter is the overview of "long-term survival" for the whole handbook: it covers
  principles, priorities, rough quantities and common mistakes; the details are in the
  specialist chapters, linked at the end of each section
- The vast majority of disasters are temporary: power cuts, water cuts, earthquakes,
  floods, epidemics. Rescue and order come back within days to weeks.
  As long as the government, rescue teams and hospitals are still working, official
  notices and the instructions of professionals come first; if someone is injured,
  call 120 first. Do not do anything drastic because of imagining "the end of the world"
- This chapter does not cover weapons. Safety comes from keeping a low profile,
  organisation, neighbours helping each other and avoiding conflict, not from confrontation
```

For household preparation for ordinary disasters, see [Disaster Response – Household emergency kit](../02-disaster-response/README.md#-family-emergency-kit) first; for short-term survival such as getting lost or injured in the wild, see [Basic Survival Skills](00-basic-survival-skills.md).

## Core principles

```
1. Safety first
   - People matter more than things: no supplies are worth a life
2. Water, food, shelter
   - Order: keeping warm/keeping cool → water → hygiene → food;
     a person is in danger of dying after a few days without water,
     but can survive several weeks without food
3. Community cooperation
   - One family cannot defend itself or grow everything it needs for a year;
     only a community of a few dozen people can divide up the work
4. Skills > supplies
   - Supplies always run out; people who can farm, purify water and give first aid
     are the long-term safeguard
5. Adapt and be flexible
   - Have a plan, but change it when the situation changes; do not take risks just to
     "stick to the original plan"
6. Keep hope
   - Groups with goals, a daily routine and people supporting one another have
     clearly higher survival rates
```

### Priorities at a glance

| Stage | Most urgent tasks | Most common fatal mistakes |
|------|-----------|-----------------|
| Day one | People's safety, caring for the injured, storing water, listening to official information | Going out blindly to look around, risking your life to grab supplies |
| First week | Hygiene (toilets, handwashing), rationing, deciding whether to evacuate or stay put, contacting neighbours | Not rationing, defecating anywhere, drinking untreated water |
| First month | A stable water source, organising watches and division of labour, starting fast-growing vegetables | Hoarding without sharing, making enemies, fires or carbon monoxide poisoning from open flames and stoves |
| 1-6 months | Grain production, fuel, record-keeping and rationing, preventing infectious disease | Eating the seed grain, growing only one crop, cutting down all the trees nearby |
| After 6 months | Community rules, seed saving, education, building up technology | No rules, knowledge held only in one or two people's heads |

## Early stage (0-30 days)

### Timeline: day one to the first month

**First hour**:
```
1. Make sure all family members are safe; if anyone is injured, stop the bleeding and
   keep the airway open first, and call 120 if you can get through
2. If you smell gas, the building is cracked or there is a fire: turn off the main gas
   valve first and leave the dangerous building
3. Turn on the radio and phone and listen for official information
   (TV, radio, text messages, community notices)
4. Store water quickly before it is cut off: fill the bath, buckets, pans and bottles
   (bath water is for flushing toilets and washing)
```

**Day one**:
```
1. Take stock: how much water, food, medicine, fuel, cash and batteries you have;
   write it down
2. Eat what is in the fridge first: after a power cut the fridge usually stays cold for
   about 4 hours and a full freezer for about 2 days;
   only then start on tins and dry food
3. Contact family members according to your family communication plan and agree a
   meeting point
4. Check how many days of medicine people with long-term conditions have left; find a
   way to keep medicines that need refrigeration, such as insulin, cool
5. Put the emergency kit by the door, ready to evacuate at any time
```

**First week**:
```
1. Ration: when you do not know when supplies will return, eat to a plan from day two;
   do not wait until you have nearly run out to start saving
2. If the sewers are not working, switch at once to an emergency toilet (two-bucket
   method); do not keep using a blocked toilet
3. Set up a handwashing point: wash hands after the toilet, before cooking and before
   eating (this is the single most effective way to prevent diarrhoea)
4. Meet your neighbours: which households have elderly people, babies or sick people,
   and who has medical, electrical or farming skills
5. Decide whether to evacuate or stay put (see below)
6. Start taking turns on watch and checking for fire at night
```

**First month**:
```
1. Find and protect a long-term water source (a well, a spring, a roof that can collect
   rainwater)
2. Get organised: choose people in charge, keep records, form groups
   (see "Rebuilding the community")
3. Grow fast-growing vegetables and sprouts (bean sprouts are ready in a few days and
   provide vitamin C)
4. Prepare fuel: collect dry firewood, at least a month's worth for cooking
5. Keep records: daily consumption, what happened, new people who joined
```

Related chapters: [First aid](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/README.md), [Emergency Communication and Finding Family – Family communication plan](../02-disaster-response/06-emergency-communication-and-family-reunification.md#before-a-disaster-family-communication-plan), [Large-scale power and water cuts](../02-disaster-response/05-man-made-disasters.md#widespread-power-and-water-cuts), [Chronic Medication Stockpile – During a power cut](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/08-chronic-medication-stockpile.md#during-a-power-cut), [Sanitation – Two-bucket method](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/03-卫生设施.md#双桶法).

### Immediate action

**Evacuate vs stay put**:
```
Evacuate if:
- Where you are is not safe
  (damaged building, threat of flood or fire, chemical or radiation contamination,
  ongoing violent conflict)
- You have a safe destination
  (relatives' or friends' home, an official shelter, and you have confirmed they can
  take you in)
- You have transport and fuel
  (or the whole family, including elderly people and children, can get there on foot)
- You have enough supplies
  (at least enough for the journey and the first few days after arriving)

Stay put if:
- It is relatively safe
- You have stores
- You can defend it
  (strong doors and windows, neighbours who can look out for each other)
- You know the area
  (you know the water sources, neighbours, hospitals and routes out)

⚠️ When the authorities order an evacuation, leave by the official route and at the
   official time; do not stay put
⚠️ "Fleeing" with no clear destination is the most dangerous: no water and nowhere to
   stay on the way, and conflict is likely
⚠️ If the household includes someone bedridden, a baby or a woman in late pregnancy,
   count the risk of evacuating them
```

**When evacuating**:
```
✓ Agree two routes and a backup meeting point in advance
✓ Take: emergency kit, documents, medicines, water, cash, phone and power bank,
  a change of clothes
✓ Turn off the main water, electricity and gas, lock the door
✓ Leave a note of where you have gone on the door or at an agreed place
  (written for family; no details for strangers to read)
✓ Move together, do not split up; travel by day, not at night
```

For evacuation routes and meeting points, see [Human-made Disasters – Evacuation](../02-disaster-response/05-man-made-disasters.md#evacuation); for the contents of an emergency kit, see [Disaster Response – Household emergency kit](../02-disaster-response/README.md#-family-emergency-kit).

### Basic needs

**Water**:
```
✓ Store (4 L per person per day)
  - Of which about 2-3 L for drinking (more in heat, with hard work, when breastfeeding),
    the rest for cooking
  - Water for washing hands, washing and flushing toilets is extra; it can be untreated
    rainwater, river water, etc. (water for handwashing and washing dishes is best
    disinfected too)
  - 2 weeks' store = about 56 L of drinking and cooking water per person;
    about 170 L for a family of 3
✓ Purification methods (boiling, filtering, chemicals)
  - Boiling: bring to a full boil, then boil for 1 more minute (3 minutes above 2000 m);
    the most reliable
  - Chlorine disinfection: 2 drops of household bleach per litre of clear water,
    4 drops for cloudy water, wait 30 minutes
  - Let cloudy water settle and filter it first, then disinfect
✓ Backup water sources (rainwater collection, nearby water)
✓ At least 2 weeks' store
  - Containers: clean, lidded, opaque, food-grade, labelled with the date
  - Tap water in sealed containers: replace about every 6 months

Are you drinking enough? Pale yellow urine in normal amounts; dark yellow urine, passing
little urine, dry mouth and dizziness mean you are short of water
❌ Do not drink: car radiator water, swimming-pool water, industrial water of unknown
   origin, seawater, urine
```

For how much water you need, see [Water Supply – How much water you need](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/10-水源工程与供水.md#需要多少水); for purifying water, see [Water Supply – Purification](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/10-水源工程与供水.md#净化-从浑水到可以喝的水) and [Basic Survival Skills – Water purification](00-basic-survival-skills.md#water-purification).

**Food**:
```
✓ Long-term stores:
  - Tinned food (several years)
  - Dry staples (rice, flour, beans)
  - Energy bars
  - Salt, sugar, oil
✓ At least 1 month's store
  - With grain as the staple, an adult needs about 0.5 kg of grain a day (about
    1700-1800 kcal, most of a day's energy needs); about 15 kg a month
✓ Rotate (first in, first out)
  - Eat from your stores in normal times and put new purchases at the back; do not store
    food, never eat it and then throw it all away when it expires

Order: fresh food from the fridge → fresh fruit and vegetables → short-life food such as
  bread and eggs → tins and dry food
✓ Ration from the start, not when you have nearly run out
✓ Make sure there are beans, a little oil and vegetables (or bean sprouts) every day;
  eating only rice and flour for months causes deficiency diseases
❌ Do not eat: tins that are bulging, leaking or badly rusted; mouldy grain or peanuts;
   wood ear fungus soaked for too long, soured rice or flour products
   (bongkrekic acid poisoning, extremely hard to treat)
```

For deficiency diseases caused by stored food, see [Nutrition and Deficiency Diseases](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/02-营养与营养缺乏病.md#为什么储备粮会让人生病); for food poisoning, see [Food Processing and Preservation – High-risk foods and food poisoning](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/01-食物加工与保存.md#高风险食品与食物中毒).

**Shelter**:
```
✓ Strengthen your home
  - Check the building for damage; do not live in a house with obvious cracks or a lean
✓ A backup refuge
  - A relative's, friend's or neighbour's home, agreed in advance
✓ Defensive preparation
  - Door locks, strengthened doors and windows, lighting (see "Safety")
✓ Keep a low profile
  - Black out windows at night so no one can see you have power, heating or stores

Keeping warm: in the cold season, all live in one room, seal draughty window gaps, use
  several layers of clothing and bedding, and insulate yourself from the floor when
  sleeping; elderly people and babies suffer most from cold
Keeping cool: shut windows and shade them by day, ventilate morning and evening;
  drink plenty, avoid working in the hottest hours
```

For long-term housing, see [Long-term Housing](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/05-长期住所.md); for temporary shelters in the wild, see [Basic Survival Skills – Building a shelter](00-basic-survival-skills.md#building-shelters).

### Safety

When order breaks down, most harm does not come from bad people but from **fire, carbon monoxide, falls, frostbite, diarrhoea and conflict with people you know**. Think about safety measures in that order.

**Defence**:
```
✓ Strengthen doors and windows
  - Doors must lock reliably from the inside; strengthen ground-floor windows
  - Do not block every exit: you must be able to get out in a fire
✓ Emergency escape routes
  - At least two ways out of every room; the whole family knows where to meet
✓ Lookout (know what is going on around you)
  - By day, notice who comes and goes and what has changed nearby
✓ Alarm system (home-made)
  - Hang bells or strings of empty tins at the door; at night put things near doors and
    windows that make a noise if knocked
✓ Stay alert
  - Take turns on watch at night: pairs, shifts of 2-4 hours; if something happens, wake
    the others first, do not deal with it alone
```

**Fire and carbon monoxide** (one of the commonest causes of death when order breaks down):
```
✓ Never leave open-flame lights or candles unattended; keep them away from curtains and
  bedding; put them all out before sleeping
✓ Do not use charcoal braziers, generators or gas grills for heating or cooking indoors,
  in tents or in garages
✓ A fire indoors must have a chimney or good ventilation
✓ Keep a few buckets of water and sand indoors; a fire extinguisher is better
✓ Store fuel (petrol, LPG, diesel) outside in a ventilated place, away from living
  quarters and sources of fire
⚠️ Headache, dizziness and nausea in several people in the same room at once
   → suspect carbon monoxide poisoning first; open windows at once and get people outside
```

**People**:
```
✓ Build a group you trust
  - Start with family, neighbours, colleagues; people in the same building or village
    are the most natural
✓ Divide up the work
✓ Look out for each other
  - Help elderly people, disabled people and neighbours with children; they get into
    difficulty first, and they will become your allies
✓ Be careful with strangers
  - Talk through the door or outside the yard; meet them as a group, not alone
  - Do not reveal how many people or how many stores you have
  - If you can, give a little water or food and tell them where the official shelter or
    relief point is
  - People who want to join: first find out what they can do and where they come from,
    then decide after everyone has discussed it

Defusing conflict:
✓ When the other person is agitated, speak calmly and give them a way to back down
✓ Stepping back and handing over some supplies in exchange for peace is often the best
  outcome
✓ If threatened with violence, hide or leave first; call the police (110) if you can
❌ Do not fight or give chase to keep your supplies
```

For carbon monoxide poisoning, see [Home and Traffic Accidents – Carbon monoxide poisoning](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/07-home-and-traffic-accidents.md#carbon-monoxide-poisoning); for fire prevention, see [Fire – Fire prevention](../02-disaster-response/04-fire.md#preventing-fires); for open-flame lighting, see [Lighting and Energy – Safety rules for open-flame lighting](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/06-照明与能源.md#使用明火照明的安全规则); for staying safe in war, see [Human-made Disasters – War and armed conflict](../02-disaster-response/05-man-made-disasters.md#war-and-armed-conflict).

## Middle stage (1-6 months)

If supplies have still not returned after a month, you need to switch from "using up stores" to "producing for yourself". Stores buy you time; the aim is to harvest the first crop before the stores run out.

### Self-sufficiency

**Priorities**:
```
1. A stable, clean water source — without water, nothing else matters
2. Hygiene: toilets, handwashing, rubbish disposal — diarrhoea is one of the biggest
   killers after a disaster
3. Fast-growing vegetables and sprouts — harvest within 1-2 months, prevents deficiency
   diseases
4. Staple grains and tubers — decide whether you survive the first winter
5. Fuel — needed for cooking, boiling water and heating; firewood must be dried in advance
6. Salt — people and livestock cannot do without it; also used to preserve food
7. Animals — chickens are easiest; start with a few
```

**Water**:
```
✓ Rainwater collection system
  - Collect from the roof, discard the first dirty water of each shower; cover storage
    barrels against mosquitoes
✓ A well (if possible)
  - Digging a well carries a risk of collapse and lack of oxygen; someone must keep
    watch at the top; never go down a well alone
✓ A nearby stream
  - Collect water upstream of the village and the toilets
✓ Purification equipment
  - Slow sand filtration + boiling or disinfection can supply a community long term
✓ Reuse (greywater)
  - Water from washing vegetables and clothes can water crops (not vegetables eaten raw)
    and flush toilets

⚠️ Toilets, animal pens and rubbish pits at least 30 m from water sources, and downhill
   of them
```

For details, see [Water Supply](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/10-水源工程与供水.md) (collecting rainwater, digging wells, slow sand filters, storage).

**Food production**:
```
✓ Growing:
  - Fast crops (radish, lettuce, pak choi, spinach: 30-60 days)
  - High-yield crops (potatoes, maize)
  - Storage crops (pumpkins, sweet potatoes)
  - Legumes (soya beans, cowpeas, peas: protein, and they improve the soil)

✓ Keeping animals:
  - Chickens (eggs, meat)
  - Rabbits (breed fast)
  - Fish (if there is water)

✓ Foraging:
  - Wild plants
  - Hunting, fishing
  - Insects

Rough needs: about 180 kg of grain per person per year (about 0.5 kg a day); several
  times more by fresh weight for tubers
Suggested planting order: potatoes, sweet potatoes → maize → soya beans → rice where
  there is water → wheat

Common mistakes:
❌ Eating the seed grain — eat the seed and there is no next year
❌ Growing only one crop — one disease, pest or natural disaster wipes everything out
❌ Saving seed from hybrid (F1) varieties — the second generation segregates and yields
   fall sharply
❌ Picking wild greens or mushrooms you do not know — there is no simple way to identify
   poisonous mushrooms; the "edibility test" is only a last resort in extreme situations
   and does not work for mushrooms
⚠️ In normal times wild animals are protected by law and hunting is forbidden; consider it
   only when no other food can be had and order has completely collapsed, and prefer
   fishing and insects
```

Related chapters: [Vegetable Growing – Fast-growing vegetables](../04-farming-and-animal-husbandry/01-vegetable-growing.md#fast-growing-vegetables-30-60-days), [Grain Crops – Recommended planting order for beginners](../04-farming-and-animal-husbandry/02-grain-crops.md#recommended-order-for-beginners), [Poultry Keeping](../04-farming-and-animal-husbandry/05-poultry.md), [Aquaculture](../04-farming-and-animal-husbandry/07-aquaculture.md), [Seed Saving and Breeding – Why hybrid seed cannot be saved](../04-farming-and-animal-husbandry/08-seed-saving-and-breeding.md#why-hybrid-f1-seed-cannot-be-saved), [Poisonous Mushrooms and Wild Plants](09-poisonous-mushrooms-and-wild-plants.md#rules-for-gathering-wild-greens-and-fruit), [Basic Survival Skills – Edible plant test](00-basic-survival-skills.md#edibility-test-for-plants).

**Energy**:
```
✓ Firewood (cooking, heating)
  - Cooking only: about 1.5-3 kg of air-dried wood per person per day; heating often
    takes far more wood than cooking
  - Wet wood is smoky and gives little heat; cut wood must be dried before burning, so
    prepare a season ahead
  - Use dead branches, fallen trees and scrap timber first; do not cut down all the
    trees around the house
✓ Solar power (if you have the equipment)
  - Use it first to charge radios, phones and lights, not for heating or cooking
✓ Human power (bicycle generators, etc.)
✓ Save heat
  - Make one fire do several jobs: boil water while cooking rice; use insulated
    containers or straw-lined boxes to finish cooking and save wood
  - In areas with a *kang* (heated brick bed), the cooking fire also heats the *kang*
```

Related chapters: [Firewood and Woodland Management – How much wood you need](../04-farming-and-animal-husbandry/09-firewood-and-woodland-management.md#how-much-firewood-you-need), [Lighting and Energy](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/06-照明与能源.md), [Long-term Housing – Kang and stoves](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/05-长期住所.md#火炕与炉灶).

### Developing skills

**Essential skills**:
```
✓ Growing crops and keeping animals
✓ Food preservation (salting, smoking, drying)
✓ Water purification
✓ First aid
✓ Basic repairs
✓ Toolmaking
✓ Navigation
✓ Security and defusing conflict

How to learn:
- Learn what "saves lives and is used every day" first: first aid, water purification,
  hygiene, cooking and preserving food
- At least two people should know each skill; do not depend on one person
- Learn by doing; let experienced older people teach younger ones (apprenticeship)
- Write down what you learn and leave it for those who come after
```

For details of food preservation, nutrition, sanitation and soap making, see [Everyday Living Skills](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/README.md). For repairs and tools, see [Woodworking and Tool Maintenance](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/09-木工与工具维护.md) and [Rope and Knots](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/17-绳索与结绳.md); for how to teach a craft, see [Teaching and Knowledge Preservation – How to teach a craft](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/12-教育与知识传承.md#怎样教手艺).

### Managing supplies

```
✓ Keep an inventory
  - For each item: name, quantity, where it is stored, date in, expiry date
  - Record everything in and out in a running ledger; count and check regularly
✓ Rationing and distribution
  - Share by need, not equally: heavy manual workers, pregnant and breastfeeding women,
    growing children and sick people get more
  - Set the rules in advance and make them public; two people hand out supplies together
    and record it on the spot
✓ Recycle and reuse
✓ Barter
  - Use common community scales and measures; use grain as the unit of value and keep a
    reference price list
  - Trade with outsiders at a fixed place outside the community; go in a group and do
    not reveal the total of your stores
✓ Use sparingly

How long will it last?
  Days available = total stock ÷ daily consumption
  Example: 300 kg of grain, 10 people, 0.5 kg per person per day → 300 ÷ 5 = 60 days
  90 days until the next harvest → start saving now, sow more fast crops, forage, trade

Common mistakes:
❌ Keeping it all in your head, not writing it down
❌ One person both holding the keys and keeping the books (mistakes are easy and
   impossible to sort out afterwards)
❌ Storing all the grain in one place (one fire or one rat infestation and it is all gone)
❌ Mixing seed grain with food grain
```

For details of record-keeping, inventory, rationing and trade, see [Record-keeping, Trade and Division of Labour](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/13-记账交易与社会分工.md) (especially [Inventory management](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/13-记账交易与社会分工.md#库存管理), [Rationing](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/13-记账交易与社会分工.md#配给) and [Trade](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/13-记账交易与社会分工.md#交易)).

## Long term (6 months+)

### Rebuilding the community

One person or one family can hold out for a few months, but to survive long term and make life steadily better, you need a community that divides up the work. History and post-disaster experience everywhere show that **organised groups fare far better than individuals each fending for themselves**.

**Organisation**:
```
✓ Set up / join a community
  - A few households to a few dozen people: can sustain farming, animal keeping, seed
    saving, woodworking, textiles, first aid and hygiene
  - Several hundred people: only then is there capacity for iron smelting and forging,
    brick making and running a school
✓ Specialised division of labour
  - Start with a skills register: what each person can do and wants to learn
  - Common groups: farming, water and sanitation, fuel and building, medical care,
    logistics and record-keeping, education
✓ Share resources
✓ Collective defence
  - Keeping watch and guarding against fire together takes far less effort than each
    household doing it alone
✓ Complementary skills
```

**Rules**:
```
✓ Basic law and order
  - Write important rules down and make them public; all adults know them
  - Examples: no washing clothes near the water source, who holds the granary key,
    how breaches are dealt with
✓ Fair distribution
  - The person who holds the keys does not keep the books, and the bookkeeper does not
    hold the keys; make the accounts public regularly
  - Elderly, sick and disabled people must not get less food because they cannot work
✓ A way to resolve conflicts
  - First hear both sides out, find someone with no stake in it to mediate, and deal with
    it by the rules agreed in advance
✓ Common goals
  - Discuss major decisions (moving, accepting new members, using seed grain) at a
    meeting and record them

Common mistakes:
❌ One person decides everything, with no oversight
❌ Rules exist only by word of mouth, so when something happens everyone tells it differently
❌ Shutting out outsiders — newcomers often bring skills the community lacks;
   vet and observe them according to the rules instead of refusing everyone
```

For details of division of labour, rules, preventing unfairness and community records, see [Record-keeping, Trade and Division of Labour](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/13-记账交易与社会分工.md) (especially [Division of labour and cooperation](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/13-记账交易与社会分工.md#分工与协作), [Rules and decision-making](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/13-记账交易与社会分工.md#规则和决策) and [Preventing mistakes and unfairness](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/13-记账交易与社会分工.md#防止错误和不公)); for the technology communities of different sizes can sustain, see [Technology Rebuilding Roadmap – What a small community can achieve](../../../docs/09-进阶科学与工程/00-技术重建路线图.md#六小社区能做到什么).

### Sustainable survival

**Farming**:
```
✓ Crop rotation
  - Do not grow the same type of crop on the same plot year after year; rotate legumes
    with grain crops
✓ Composting
  - Human excreta and animal manure must be thoroughly composted or stored for a long
    time before use
✓ Seed saving
  - Save enough seed of every crop, store it separately, keep separate records, and
    never eat it
  - Keep at least two years' seed, stored in two places, so one failure does not wipe
    out a variety
✓ Diverse planting
✓ Seasonal planning
  - Time sowing by local natural signs and the solar terms; record each year's weather
    and harvest and improve year by year
```

**Animal keeping**:
```
✓ Breeding management
  - Choose healthy, productive animals for breeding; avoid long-term inbreeding
✓ Health care
  - Do not eat animals that died of disease; bury them deep; quarantine and observe
    newly brought-in animals
✓ Feed self-sufficiency
  - Do not keep more animals than you can feed: animals that compete with people for
    grain are a burden
```

**Preserving technology**:
```
✓ Books (practical skills)
  - Keep paper copies of this handbook, and of books on farming, medicine and secondary-
    school maths, physics and chemistry, as a priority
✓ Tool maintenance
  - Clean and oil tools after use and store them dry; do not throw away broken ones,
    the metal can be reused
✓ Passing on knowledge
  - Children learn reading, arithmetic, hygiene and safety first, then crafts
✓ Recording experience
  - Make several handwritten copies of important material and store them in separate places
```

### Health

Without hospitals, **prevention matters far more than treatment**: in famines and wars throughout history, most people who died were killed by diarrhoea, respiratory infections, malnutrition and wound infections, and most of these can be prevented by hygiene and nutrition.

**Prevention**:
```
✓ Hygiene (handwashing, cleaning)
  - Wash hands with soap after the toilet, before cooking, before eating and after
    caring for the sick; without soap, use plant ash (wood ash)
✓ Food safety
  - Keep raw and cooked food apart, reheat leftovers thoroughly; do not eat mouldy or
    spoiled food
✓ Water purification
✓ Rubbish disposal
  - Compost kitchen waste; bury other rubbish in a pit away from water and living quarters
✓ Control insects and rats
  - Remove standing water against mosquitoes; seal grain against rats
✓ Nutrition
  - Iodised salt, legumes, and vegetables or sprouts are all essential; take special care
    of pregnant women, babies and young children, and elderly people
✓ Work safely
  - Felling trees, digging wells and going up on roofs cause the most injuries; plan the
    safety measures first and do not work alone
```

**Medical care**:
```
✓ First-aid skills
  - At least half of all adults can stop bleeding, do CPR, treat scalds and burns and
    splint fractures
✓ Medicine stores
  - Use common over-the-counter medicines as the package leaflet says; build up
    prescription medicines for long-term conditions gradually, as prescribed by a doctor
✓ Herbal knowledge
  - Use only medicinal plants you can identify with certainty and have reliable
    information on; check the method and dose before using them
✓ Isolate the sick
✓ Basic nursing
  - Bringing down fever, giving fluids, turning to prevent pressure sores, feeding and
    keeping clean can save many lives

⚠️ As long as you can still reach a hospital, doctor or health centre, take the patient
   there; do not struggle on by yourself
⚠️ Babies, pregnant women and elderly people get worse fast; lower your threshold:
   spot problems early, act early
```

**Mental health**:
```
✓ Keep a regular routine
✓ Recreation
✓ Support each other
✓ Keep hope
✓ Set goals
```

## Special situations

### Nuclear disaster

In a nuclear or radiation accident, the main danger to the public is **radioactive dust (fallout)**. The key is **time, distance, shielding**: the shorter you stay, the further away you are and the thicker the walls and earth between you, the less radiation you receive.

```
Early stage:
✓ Take shelter (basement, inner room)
  - Go indoors at once: ideally a basement, or a room in the middle of a building, away
    from outer walls and the roof
    (the top floor is close to fallout on the roof, the ground floor close to fallout on
    the ground)
  - People outdoors should get into the nearest solid building as soon as possible;
    wooden huts and cars give very little shielding
✓ Seal doors and windows
  - Turn off air conditioning, extractor fans, fresh-air systems and cooker hoods
✓ Listen for information
✓ Stored food and water
  - Water and food that were sealed before the accident can be eaten; do not drink
    outdoor rainwater or uncovered water
✓ Do not go out
  - Be prepared to stay at least 24 hours; fallout is most dangerous in the first few
    hours and its intensity falls quickly over time; do not go out to look unless told to
  - Rough rule: the radiation from nuclear-explosion fallout falls to about 1/10 after
    about 7 hours and to about 1/100 after about 2 days

Middle stage:
✓ Follow official guidance
  - Whether to evacuate or keep sheltering in place depends on the notice for your area;
    when evacuating, do not go downwind
✓ Avoid contaminated food and water
  - Do not eat vegetables from the garden, fruit from the trees, free-range poultry and
    livestock, or milk and eggs until they have been tested
  - Boiling and ordinary water filters do not remove radioactive substances
✓ Decontaminate (undress, shower)
  - Before going inside, take off outer clothes and shoes, seal them in a bag and put it
    well away (this removes most of the contamination)
  - Shower gently, use shampoo but not conditioner; if you cannot shower, wipe exposed
    skin and hair with a damp towel
✓ Iodine tablets (under official guidance)
  - Take them only when officially told to; do not take them "preventively" on your own
  - They only stop the thyroid absorbing radioactive iodine; they do not protect against
    other radiation
  - ❌ Do not eat iodised salt or drink iodine tincture or povidone-iodine instead of
    iodine tablets (you will get salt poisoning or be poisoned)

Long term:
✓ Monitor radiation
✓ Keep away from highly contaminated areas
✓ Regular health checks
  - Nausea, vomiting, weakness or reddened skin within a few hours of the accident:
    tell medical staff at once
✓ Psychological support
  - Fear of radiation and uncertainty often affect more people than the radiation itself
```

For the full procedure (steps for sheltering in place, evacuation, what to do in a nuclear explosion, what iodine tablets do and who must not take them), [Human-made Disasters – Nuclear radiation](../02-disaster-response/05-man-made-disasters.md#nuclear-radiation) is authoritative; for the physics of radiation dose, see [Modern Physics – Radiation dose](../../../docs/09-进阶科学与工程/06-现代物理.md#64-辐射剂量).

### Epidemics

```
✓ Isolate the sick
  - The sick person stays in a separate room; if there is none, keep more than 1 m apart
    and hang a curtain between
  - One fixed person does the caring (preferably not an elderly person, a pregnant woman
    or someone with a long-term condition)
✓ Personal protection (masks, gloves)
  - Wear gloves when touching the sick person's secretions, vomit or faeces; wash hands
    straight after removing gloves
✓ Disinfection
  - Disinfect frequently touched surfaces every day; wash the sick person's clothes and
    bedding separately, in hot water if possible
✓ Avoid contact
  - Cut down on gatherings during an outbreak; newcomers stay in separate quarters under
    observation for about 2 weeks before entering the community
✓ Strengthen immunity (nutrition, rest)
✓ Care for the sick (with proper protection)
  - For people with diarrhoea the most important thing is prompt fluid replacement
    (oral rehydration salts); many die of dehydration rather than the disease itself

At community level:
✓ Keep records: who has fever, diarrhoea or a rash each day, to catch the first signs of
  an outbreak early
✓ Water sources and toilets are the key to preventing gut infections; cholera, typhoid
  and dysentery are all "swallowed"
✓ Handle the bodies of the dead with the required protection; but bodies from ordinary
  disasters do not usually cause epidemics, so do not bury or burn them hastily out of panic
⚠️ When you can reach medical services and the disease control authorities, isolate and
   treat as they require
```

For details, see [Infectious Disease at Home](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/15-infectious-disease-at-home.md) (especially [Home isolation](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/15-infectious-disease-at-home.md#isolating-at-home), [Making household disinfectant](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/15-infectious-disease-at-home.md#making-household-disinfectant) and [Diarrhoeal infections](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/15-infectious-disease-at-home.md#diarrhoeal-infections)); for handling bodies, see [Handling the Dead after a Disaster – People who died of infectious disease](../02-disaster-response/07-handling-the-dead-after-disasters.md#people-who-died-of-infectious-diseases).

### Social collapse

```
✓ Keep a low profile (do not show wealth)
  - Do not eat outside or show off your stores; cooking smoke and smells also attract
    attention
✓ Look out for each other
✓ Avoid conflict
  - Talk where you can, give way where you can; an injury from a fight may be fatal
    when there are no hospitals
✓ Stay mobile (able to evacuate quickly)
  - Keep the emergency kit by the door at all times and fuel in the car (if you have one)
✓ Several hiding places
  - Store supplies in different places: if one is found or destroyed, there are others
✓ Build a network of trust
  - Stay in touch with several nearby communities to share news, trade supplies and
    help each other in emergencies
```

## Stockpile list

Prepare in order: **3 days → 2 weeks → 3 months**. Most households should have a 3-day emergency kit; build up larger stores gradually as you can afford them, eat and replace them in normal times, and do not hoard large amounts all at once or panic-buy when disaster strikes.

### Basic stores (3-6 months)

**Quantities per person** (adults, 3 months; a little less for children and elderly people, more for heavy manual work and breastfeeding):

| Item | Per person per day, about | Per person for 3 months, about | Notes |
|------|-----------|------------|------|
| Drinking and cooking water | 4 L (about 2-3 L for drinking) | 2 weeks' store about 56 L | For longer, rely on water sources and purification, not stores |
| Grain (rice, flour, other grains) | 0.5 kg | 45 kg | Do not store only polished white rice; store some brown rice and other grains |
| Legumes | 50 g | 4.5 kg | Soya beans, mung beans; can be sprouted for vitamin C |
| Cooking oil | 25 g | 2.5 L | Keep dark and cool; goes rancid after its date |
| Iodised salt | 5 g | 0.5 kg | Store extra for preserving food |
| Sugar | A little | 1 kg | Can also be used for oral rehydration |

## 📝 Long-term collapse survival key points

```
Short term:
✓ Safety first
✓ Basic needs (water, food, shelter)
✓ Assess the situation
✓ Conserve resources

Middle term:
✓ Self-sufficiency (growing crops, keeping animals)
✓ Learning skills
✓ Community cooperation
✓ Resource management

Long term:
✓ Sustainable farming
✓ Passing on knowledge
✓ Rebuilding the community
✓ Preserving civilisation

Key numbers:
- Water: about 2-3 L of drinking water per person per day; store 4 L per day, for at
  least 2 weeks
- Grain: about 0.5 kg per adult per day, for at least 1 month; 3 months if you can
- Toilets and animal pens at least 30 m from water sources
- Boil for 1 minute (3 minutes above 2000 m)

Core:
- Preparation > reaction
- Skills > supplies
- Cooperation > going it alone
- Adapting > holding fast
- Hope > despair
```

## Psychological preparation

```
Psychological challenges of a collapse:
- Fear, uncertainty
- Loss, grief
- Stress, anxiety
- Loneliness

Normal reactions: in the first few weeks after a disaster, sleeplessness, nightmares,
being easily startled, numbness and irritability; for most people these ease slowly
over time. This is not weakness

Coping:
✓ Keep a regular routine
  - Fixed times for getting up, eating, working and resting give a sense that
    "life goes on"
✓ Support each other
  - Spend some time every day sitting together and talking; do not push people to talk
    about what happened, but listen properly when they want to
✓ Set goals
  - Small and specific: "fix the water bucket this week", "finish planting this plot by
    the end of the month"
✓ Celebrate small victories
✓ Stay humane
  - Hold the line: look after the weak, keep your promises, respect the dead
✓ Keep hope
✓ Record history
✓ Protect children
  - Children's sense of safety comes from adults: keep them with family as much as
    possible and keep up play, lessons and daily routines
✓ Pass on culture
  - Festivals, storytelling, singing, teaching children to read: these are the things
    that keep people going

People to watch closely:
⚠️ Not eating or sleeping for a long time, not speaking, not interested in anything
⚠️ Saying "life isn't worth living" or "everyone would be better off without me", or
   starting to put their affairs in order or give away treasured possessions
→ Do not avoid it; ask directly and gently; stay with them, do not leave them alone,
  remove dangerous items, and contact professionals as soon as you can

People who care for others must look after themselves too: take turns to rest, and say
so when you are exhausted

Remember:
Humans are resilient!
History has seen countless rebuildings
Keep faith and keep acting
```

For details of psychological first aid, recognising suicide risk, grief and caring for children's mental health, see [Mental Health](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/10-mental-health.md) (especially [Psychological first aid](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/10-mental-health.md#psychological-first-aid-how-to-help-people-around-you), [Suicide risk](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/10-mental-health.md#suicide-risk-recognition-and-response), [Caring for children's mental health](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/10-mental-health.md#psychological-care-for-children) and [Looking after yourself under long-term stress](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/10-mental-health.md#looking-after-yourself-under-long-term-stress)).
