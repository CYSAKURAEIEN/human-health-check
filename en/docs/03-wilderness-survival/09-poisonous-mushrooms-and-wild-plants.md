## Why this needs its own chapter

**Eating wild-picked poisonous mushrooms is the leading cause of household food poisoning in China, and the main cause of deaths from food poisoning.** Every rainy season (spring and summer in the south, summer and autumn in the north), there are cases in Yunnan, Guizhou, Hunan, Sichuan, Guangxi and elsewhere of whole families being poisoned and several people dying. Mushrooms growing in city parks and on residential lawns are just as poisonous.

Mistakenly eating wild greens or wild fruit is less common, but a few plants are extremely toxic, and eating a little can be fatal.

```
The single most important rule:
✓ Do not pick, buy, sell or eat wild mushrooms you do not know
✓ "Knowing" means 100% certain; "it looks like…" and "I've eaten something similar
  before" do not count
✓ In a survival situation, mushrooms have very few calories and are not worth the risk
```

## Poisonous mushrooms: there is no simple test

Many deadly mushrooms look very like edible ones, and even experienced mushroom pickers are poisoned every year. **There is currently no simple way to tell whether a mushroom is poisonous; identification needs experts and equipment.**

```
All of the following "rules of thumb" are unreliable:
❌ "Brightly coloured ones are poisonous, plain ones are safe"
   → many of the deadliest Amanita mushrooms are white, grey or greyish-brown, and look
     very plain
❌ "Cook them with a silver needle, silverware or garlic; if it turns black they are
   poisonous"
   → mushroom toxins do not make silver or garlic change colour
❌ "Cooking them well, cooking them longer or adding chilli removes the poison"
   → the deadly toxins (amatoxins) are very stable and are not destroyed by heat
❌ "If insects or animals have eaten them, they are safe"
   → animals and people react differently to the toxins
❌ "Ones growing under pine trees or on grass are safe"
❌ "Only ones that ooze milk or change colour when torn are poisonous"
❌ "Identify them with a phone photo app"
   → identification accuracy is low; it cannot be used to decide whether they are edible
❌ "The old folk in the village have always eaten them"
   → many of those poisoned every year have been picking mushrooms for years
```

### Especially dangerous groups

```
Amanita mushrooms (commonly called "white death cap", "deadly white death cap",
"grey-streaked amanita", etc.):
- The main cause of deaths from mushroom poisoning in China
- Many are white or grey and look plain; often mistaken for edible wild mushrooms
- Poisoning is followed by a "false recovery" period, then liver failure (see next section)

Lepiota brunneoincarnata: common in parks and green spaces in northern cities; deadly,
  and also damages the liver

Russula subnigricans: common in the south; looks like edible wild mushrooms,
  can cause rhabdomyolysis (muscle breakdown); the death rate is very high

Jianshouqing ("turns blue when touched") and other boletes: sold in markets, but if not
  thoroughly stir-fried they cause hallucinations (seeing "little people", etc.);
  they must be heated right through
```

⚠️ Do not try to "identify" mushrooms from the descriptions above. They are here only to show that deadly mushrooms often do not "look poisonous" the way people imagine.

## Types of mushroom poisoning

| Type | Usual time to symptoms | Signs | Danger |
|------|---------------------|------|----------|
| Gastrointestinal | Fairly quick, often within a few hours | Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhoea | Mostly mild, but severe diarrhoea can cause dehydration |
| Neuropsychiatric | Fairly quick | Hallucinations, agitation, drooling, heavy sweating, pupil changes | Most recover; can be life-threatening when severe |
| Acute liver damage | **Slow, often after 6-30 hours** | First vomiting and diarrhoea; 1-2 days after improving, jaundice, bleeding, coma | **Most deadly** |
| Rhabdomyolysis | A few hours to a day | Aching, weak muscles; urine the colour of soy sauce | Very dangerous; can cause kidney failure and cardiac arrest |
| Haemolytic, acute kidney failure, etc. | Varies | Darker urine, passing little urine | Dangerous |

### ⚠️ False recovery

```
Typical course of acute liver-damage poisoning (mainly caused by Amanita mushrooms):
1. 6-30 hours after eating, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain and diarrhoea begin
2. After treatment the symptoms go and the patient feels "better" — this is the
   "false recovery"
3. 1-2 days later, signs of liver and kidney failure appear: jaundice, bleeding, coma;
   by then the best time for treatment has often been missed

So:
⚠️ Symptoms that start more than 6 hours after eating mushrooms are actually MORE dangerous
⚠️ Do not skip hospital or leave hospital early because "I've been sick and had
   diarrhoea and now I'm fine"
```

## Feeling unwell after eating wild mushrooms

```
1. Call 120 at once or get to hospital as soon as possible; do not wait for the
   symptoms to get worse
2. Everyone who ate the mushrooms must go to hospital too, even if they have no symptoms
3. Bring any leftover mushrooms (raw or cooked), or take photos
   - If none are left, bring the dish that was eaten or the vomit
   - This helps the doctor work out which mushroom it was
4. Tell the doctor: when you ate them, how much, and when you started to feel unwell
5. Even if the symptoms improve, do as the doctor says; do not discharge yourself

❌ Do not try home remedies to "detoxify" (drinking mung bean soup, swallowing vinegar, etc.)
❌ Do not make yourself vomit; whether to induce vomiting is for 120 or the doctor to decide
```

For general management of poisoning, see [Poisoning – General principles of managing poisoning](../../../docs/01-日常疾病和伤害/12-中毒.md#中毒的通用处理原则).

## Poisonous plants that are easily mistaken

| Poisonous plant | Often mistaken for | Danger |
|----------|-----------|------|
| *Gouwen* (Gelsemium elegans, "heartbreak grass") | Honeysuckle, other herbs | Extremely toxic; a small amount can kill by paralysing breathing |
| Water hemlock (Cicuta virosa) | Wild celery, water dropwort (Chinese celery) | Whole plant poisonous; convulsions, paralysed breathing |
| Pokeweed root (Phytolacca) | Ginseng | Vomiting, diarrhoea, convulsions; paralysed breathing when severe |
| Seeds, fruit and flowers of thorn apple (Datura) | Gets into food by mistake, mistaken for a herb | Dry mouth, flushed skin, dilated pupils, hallucinations, frenzy |
| Fruit of *masang* (Coriaria) | Wild berries (children often eat them) | Convulsions, coma |
| Castor beans | Nuts | Severe vomiting and diarrhoea, dehydration; can be fatal |
| Aconite: *wutou*, *caowu*, *fuzi* (Aconitum) | Home-picked, home-made medicinal wine or stews | Heart rhythm disturbance, cardiac arrest; extremely dangerous |
| Bitter apricot kernels, peach kernels, raw cassava | Nuts, food | Contain cyanide; poisoning when eaten raw or in quantity |

```
✓ These are only common examples; there are far more poisonous plants than these
✓ Do not pick your own herbs in the mountains or make your own medicinal wine; buy
  Chinese medicinal materials from a proper pharmacy and use them as the doctor directs
✓ Teach children not to eat fruit or seeds from the roadside or the wild
```

Other poisonous plants that get mistaken include oleander and poison ivy; see [Forest Survival – Plants](../../../docs/03-野外生存/02-森林求生.md#植物).

For how to tell *Gouwen* from honeysuckle, water hemlock from water dropwort, pokeweed from ginseng and other easily confused poisonous plants, see [Toxic Chinese Medicinals and Easily Confused Poisonous Plants](../../../docs/10-中药材识别与采集/09-有毒中药与易混淆有毒植物.md#四易混淆的有毒植物对照).

## Rules for gathering wild greens and fruit

```
Only eat:
✓ What you are 100% sure you know (ideally something you have eaten since childhood
  and that is commonly eaten locally)
✓ What has been prepared by the local traditional method (many wild greens must be
  blanched or soaked to remove bitterness and harmful substances)

Do not pick:
❌ Anything you do not know or are not sure of
❌ From roadsides, near factories, beside rubbish heaps, or where pesticide has been sprayed

When eating:
✓ The first time you eat a particular wild green, eat only a little
✓ If after eating wild greens or fruit your mouth or tongue goes numb, or you feel sick
  or dizzy → stop at once and get medical help
✓ Keep a little uneaten, so that if you are poisoned you can show it to the doctor
```

### About the "edibility test"

Some survival guides describe a step-by-step "universal edibility test". **This test cannot detect slow-acting toxins** (the toxins of poisonous mushrooms and some plants take hours to days to act), **and it does not work at all for mushrooms**. People can go several weeks without food, but eating a highly toxic plant once can kill. See the warning in [Basic Survival Skills – Edible plant test](../../../docs/03-野外生存/00-基础生存技能.md#可食植物测试).

More reliable sources of food in the wild are insects, fish and plants you can identify with certainty; see [Basic Survival Skills – Finding food](../../../docs/03-野外生存/00-基础生存技能.md#食物获取).

## 📝 Key points

```
1. Do not pick, buy or eat wild mushrooms you do not know
2. Colour, silver needles, garlic, insect bites, cooking — all these tests are unreliable
3. The deadliest Amanita mushrooms look plain, and poisoning has a "false recovery"
4. Symptoms that start more than 6 hours after eating are more dangerous
5. After poisoning, everyone who ate them goes to hospital, bringing leftover mushrooms
   or photos
6. *Gouwen* looks like honeysuckle, water hemlock like water dropwort, pokeweed root
   like ginseng
7. Do not pick your own herbs to make medicinal wine (aconite, *wutou* and *caowu*, is
   extremely dangerous)
8. Eat only wild greens you know 100% and that have been prepared by the traditional method
```
