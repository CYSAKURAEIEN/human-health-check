## Flash floods: the biggest danger in stream valleys

Mountain stream valleys collect water fast. **Heavy rain upstream, clear blue sky downstream** — and yet the flood comes down within minutes. Every year people playing in the water or camping in river valleys are swept away by flash floods.

### Signs that a flash flood is coming

```
⚠️ If any of these happen, leave the channel at once and head for high ground:
- The stream suddenly turns muddy or yellow
- The water level suddenly rises, or unusually drops all of a sudden (it may be blocked
  upstream and about to burst through)
- Lots of branches, grass or foam suddenly appear in the water
- A rumbling roar is heard from upstream
- Dark clouds and thunder upstream, or heavy rain has already been falling for a while
- You receive a heavy-rain or flash-flood warning
```

### Caught by a flash flood

```
✓ Run at once to high ground on either side of the channel (at right angles to the flow)
❌ Do not run downstream along the valley (you cannot outrun the flood)
❌ Do not go back for your belongings
❌ Do not wade across the river; do not drive across flooded roads or bridges
✓ If trapped, climb to high ground or into a large, sturdy tree and wait for rescue
✓ Flash floods often go down quickly, but do not go back into the channel until the
  rain has stopped
```

For more on floods, see [Weather Disasters – Floods](../../../docs/02-灾难应对/02-气象灾害.md#洪水).

## Choosing a campsite

```
❌ Do not camp:
- On river beaches, in the channel, or in a dry riverbed (a riverbed that looks dry can
  become a river again after rain)
- At the mouth of a gully or the bottom of a gorge
- In low ground where two streams meet
- Below cliffs or steep slopes (rockfall)
- Under an isolated large tree (lightning, falling branches)

✓ Choose:
- A terrace clearly higher than the riverbed, with no signs of water having washed over
  the ground (mud and silt, flattened grass, debris caught in trees)
- Some distance from the water's edge, with a route to escape to higher ground
✓ When it rains at night, someone listens to the sound of the water and watches the
  level, ready to leave at any moment
```

For more on choosing a shelter site, see [Basic Survival Skills – Choosing a site](../../../docs/03-野外生存/00-基础生存技能.md#选址).

## Wading across rivers

**If you can avoid crossing, do not cross.** Wading is one of the most underestimated dangers in the wild: fast water about 15 cm deep can knock a person over, you can barely stand in knee-deep fast water, and once you are knocked over your rucksack drags you under.

If you need to cross the same river regularly over a long period, see [Vehicles, Boats and Roads](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/18-车船与道路.md#桥) for building a bridge, making a raft or setting up a cable ferry.

### Deciding whether you can cross

```
❌ Do not cross if:
- The water is fast: a stick thrown in floats away faster than a brisk walk
- Fast water is deeper than your knees
- The water is muddy and you cannot see the bottom
- There is a waterfall, rapids, a deep pool or fallen trees not far downstream
- It has just rained heavily and the water is rising
- You are very tired or very cold

✓ If unsure: wait
  - Mountain streams swollen by rain often drop again within a few hours
  - Rivers fed by snow and ice melt usually carry less water in the morning than in
    the afternoon
✓ Look along the bank for a better place, or go round
```

### Choosing where to cross

```
✓ Where the river is wide (usually shallower and slower)
✓ Where the surface is smooth and the bottom is small pebbles
❌ Do not choose narrow places (deep, fast water) or the outside of a bend
   (scoured deep)
❌ Do not jump across on slippery wet stones; do not walk on wet fallen logs
```

### How to cross

```
1. Undo the hip belt and chest strap of your rucksack
   (if you are knocked over you can shed the rucksack quickly and it will not drag you
   under)
2. Keep your shoes on (so your feet are not cut or trapped by stones); you can take your
   socks off
3. Face upstream and shuffle sideways in small steps
4. Plant a trekking pole or sturdy stick on the upstream side to make "three points of
   support"
   - Move only one point of support at a time, and stand firm before moving the next
5. Angle slightly downstream as you go (going with the force of the water is easier
   than against it)

Crossing as a group:
✓ Two or three people link arms or hold each other's rucksacks, line up one behind the
  other in the direction of the flow, all facing upstream, and shuffle across together
  sideways (the person upstream breaks the current for those behind)
✓ The strongest person stands furthest upstream
✓ Have someone ready downstream to catch anyone swept away

❌ Do not tie a rope to the person crossing
   - If they are knocked over, the rope will hold them under water; this is very dangerous
❌ Do not run or jump in the water
```

## Falling into fast water

```
1. Shed your rucksack
2. Lie on your back with your feet downstream, feet raised near the surface
   - Push off rocks ahead with your feet
   - Scull with your arms at your sides to steer
3. When you get the chance, swim at an angle towards the bank (at an angle to the
   current, not straight against it)
4. Stand up only once you reach slow, shallow water

❌ Do not try to stand up and walk in fast water
   - Your foot may get stuck between rocks, and the current will push you over and hold
     you under; it is very hard to get free
❌ Do not cling to fallen trees or branches in the water (the water will sweep you
   underneath them)
```

### Rescuing a companion from the water

```
✓ Protect yourself first: rescue from the bank, do not go into the water
✓ Shout loudly to tell them to drift towards the bank
✓ Throw: a rope, a lifebuoy, an empty water bottle, a rucksack (anything that floats)
✓ Reach: a branch, a trekking pole, a long strip of knotted clothing
  (lie down on the bank to lower your centre of gravity so you are not pulled in)
❌ Do not go into the water to rescue someone unless you have professional rescue training
```

For first aid once a drowning person is out of the water, see [Acute Symptoms – Drowning](../../../docs/01-日常疾病和伤害/05-急性症状.md#溺水).

## Falling into cold water

Even in summer, the water in mountain streams and reservoirs can be very cold. Cold water first makes you inhale water; it does not freeze you to death straight away.

```
The "1-10-1" principle:
- First 1 minute: falling into cold water makes you gasp uncontrollably ("cold shock")
  → do not struggle; keep your head above water as best you can, control your breathing
     and wait for it to pass
- The next 10 minutes or so: you can still move your arms and legs
  → use this time to save yourself: climb onto something floating, hold on to anything
     that floats; swim only if the bank is very close and you are sure you can make it
     (swimming in cold water loses heat faster and uses up your strength fast)
- Within about 1 hour: your body temperature falls and you gradually lose consciousness
  → if you cannot get out of the water, hold on to something floating; in a lifejacket,
     pull your knees up to your chest and hug them to reduce heat loss
     (without a lifejacket this position makes you sink; float on your back instead)
     several people in the water huddle together

Once out: take off wet clothes at once, put on dry clothes, and treat for hypothermia
as below
```

For cold-water survival positions, see [Ocean Disasters – Keeping warm while waiting for rescue](../../../docs/02-灾难应对/03-海洋灾害.md#等待救援时保持体温).

## Not only in winter: hypothermia outdoors

**Hypothermia does not only happen in snow.** At an air temperature in the teens, **rain soaking your clothes + strong wind + exhaustion** together can cause fatal hypothermia just the same. In the May 2021 mountain ultramarathon in Baiyin, Gansu, runners were hit by hail, freezing rain and strong wind; 21 competitors died, many of them wearing only short sleeves and shorts.

### When hypothermia is likely

```
- Sudden weather changes (temperature drop, rain, wind) in spring and autumn, or in
  summer high in the mountains
- Wearing cotton clothes soaked by rain or sweat
- No warm layer and no waterproof layer
- Exhausted after long exertion, not having eaten
- After falling in or wading through water
- An unplanned night outdoors
```

### Recognising it: early detection matters most

```
Early:
- Constant shivering
- Clumsy fingers, cannot do up a zip or tie shoelaces
- Stumbling, cannot keep up with the group
- Slurred speech, slower reactions, low or irritable mood
- Poorer judgement (e.g. not putting on clothes when they should)

⚠️ Danger signs:
- Shivering stops but the person becomes more confused (this is not getting better,
  it is getting worse)
- Confused, cannot be woken
- Some people start "paradoxical undressing": they feel hot and take their clothes off

⚠️ People with hypothermia often do not realise it themselves; companions must watch
   each other
```

### Treatment in the wild

```
1. Stop; do not keep walking or climbing
2. Find shelter from wind and rain (behind a big rock, in the woods, rig a tarp)
3. Take off wet clothes and put on dry ones; if there are no dry clothes, put windproof,
   waterproof clothing on over the wet clothes
4. Insulate from the ground: sit on a rucksack, sleeping mat or branches, not directly
   on the ground
5. Wrap up: wrap the person from head to toe in a sleeping bag or emergency survival
   blanket (a lot of heat is lost from the head; put a hat on)
6. If the person is alert and can swallow on their own, give warm sweet drinks and
   high-energy food
7. Wrap a hot-water bottle or a bottle of hot water in clothing and place it on the
   chest, armpits or groin (not directly against the skin)

⚠️ Severe hypothermia (shivering has stopped, confused):
- Call for help at once
- Move them gently and keep them lying flat
- Do not rub their hands and feet; do not let them walk or move about
- Do not warm them directly by a fire; do not put them in hot water
- Keep them warm and wait for rescue

❌ Do not drink alcohol (it speeds up heat loss)
```

For the stages of hypothermia and more on treatment, see [Acute Symptoms – Hypothermia](../../../docs/01-日常疾病和伤害/05-急性症状.md#失温症); for dressing for cold conditions, see [Snow and Polar Survival – Keeping warm](../../../docs/03-野外生存/04-雪地极地求生.md#保暖-最关键).

### Prevention

```
✓ Do not wear cotton inner layers; wear quick-drying fabric or wool
✓ Dress in layers like an onion: wicking base layer + warm layer + windproof,
  waterproof outer layer
✓ However good the forecast, take a warm layer and a raincoat into the mountains
✓ Take layers off before you get hot (to sweat less), and put them on as soon as you
  stop to rest
✓ Eat and drink at regular times; do not wait until you are hungry and tired
✓ If the weather suddenly changes, descend in good time or shelter where you are;
  do not push on
```

## 📝 Key points

```
1. Stream turning muddy, sudden rise, debris in the water, roar upstream
   → run at once to high ground on either side
2. Do not camp on river beaches, in dry riverbeds or at gully mouths
3. If you can avoid crossing a river, do not cross; when crossing, undo your rucksack
   hip belt, face upstream, three points of support
4. Do not tie a rope to the person crossing
5. Swept into fast water: on your back, feet downstream; do not stand up in fast water
6. Fallen into cold water: control your breathing first, use the first 10 minutes to
   save yourself
7. Rain + wind + fatigue can cause hypothermia even in the teens; no cotton, carry a
   warm layer and a raincoat
8. Shivering stopped and confusion mean severe hypothermia: move gently, keep warm,
   call for help
```
