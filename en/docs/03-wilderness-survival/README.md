## 🔑 Survival priorities

```
3 minutes - without air
3 hours   - without shelter in a harsh environment (hypothermia or heatstroke)
3 days    - without water
3 weeks   - without food
```

So in the wild, **keeping warm (or cool) is more urgent than finding water, and finding water is more urgent than finding food**. For the order of survival tasks and the survival mindset, see [Basic Survival Skills – Survival priorities](00-basic-survival-skills.md#survival-priorities).

## ⚠️ The most important points

### Before you set out

- Tell someone who is not coming your plans, and agree "if you cannot reach me by a certain time, call the police"
- Use only open, official routes; check the weather, set a turnaround time, and be off the mountain before dark
- Even on a day hike, carry a head torch, a warm layer, a raincoat, a power bank and a whistle

### When lost or injured

- If you are lost, stop first; do not follow a stream valley downhill
- If you have signal, call for help, give your latitude and longitude or send your location, then stay where you are
- Suspected spinal injury, broken thigh, reduced consciousness, shock → wait where you are for rescue; do not force a carry

### Water and weather

- Stream suddenly turns muddy, rises, carries debris, or there is a roar upstream → run at once to high ground on either side
- Do not camp on river beaches, in dry riverbeds or at the mouths of gullies; do not cross rivers unless you must
- Rain + wind + exhaustion can cause hypothermia even at 10-odd degrees; do not wear cotton clothing

### Things you eat and things that bite

- Do not pick or eat wild mushrooms you do not know; colour, silver needles, garlic and all other identification tests are unreliable
- Keep away from wasps' nests; if stung in more than 10 places or showing signs of allergy, get medical help at once
- Tuck your trousers in tightly before going into long grass, and check your whole body for ticks afterwards

## 🎒 Wilderness survival kit

### Ten essentials

1. **Knife**: multi-tool knife
2. **Fire**: lighter, waterproof matches, ferro rod
3. **Container**: a metal water bottle you can boil water in
4. **Shelter**: tarpaulin, emergency foil blanket
5. **Cord**: paracord
6. **Navigation**: compass, map (in addition to offline maps on your phone)
7. **First aid kit**: for a list, see [Treating and Evacuating the Sick and Injured in the Wild – Outdoor first aid kit](11-wilderness-casualty-care-and-evacuation.md#outdoor-first-aid-kit)
8. **Signals**: whistle, signal mirror, head torch
9. **Food**: high-energy emergency food
10. **Water purification**: purification tablets or a water filter

For day-hike kit and clothing, see [Trip Preparation and Calling for Help – Kit](07-trip-preparation-and-calling-for-help.md#equipment).
