> This chapter is based on public guidance from China's Ministry of Emergency Management, the national civil air defence authorities and the Ministry of Ecology and Environment (National Nuclear Safety Administration), together with mainstream international civil-defence advice. In a real incident, **the instructions of the local government and the rescuers on the scene always come first**. If anyone is hurt, call 120 first; for violence or anything suspicious, call 110; for fires and hazardous-chemical accidents, call 119.

## War and armed conflict

### Before a conflict

**Gathering information**:
```
✓ Follow the news
  - Trust only official channels and reliable media; check anything on social media before passing it on
✓ Register with your embassy or consulate
  - Abroad: register your details through the Ministry of Foreign Affairs consular service,
    and note the address and phone number of the Chinese embassy or consulate in that country
✓ Know your evacuation routes
  - At least two, in different directions; walk them once, and note bridges, tunnels and other places likely to be closed
✓ Prepare an emergency kit
  - Water, food, medicines, lighting, a radio and a power bank for the whole family for at least 3 days
✓ Back up important documents
  - ID cards, passports, household register (hukou book), property and bank records, medical records, prescriptions
  - Carry the paper originals in a waterproof bag; keep an electronic copy in the cloud or on a USB stick
✓ Keep cash
  - Electronic payment does not work when the power or network is down; keep some small notes, split between different places
```

**Emergency plan**:
```
✓ Safe meeting places
  - One near home and one outside the city; everyone in the family knows them, including the children
✓ Several evacuation routes
✓ An out-of-area contact
  - Choose a relative or friend living elsewhere as the family's "message relay"; after any separation, everyone reports to them that they are safe
✓ Several ways to communicate
  - Mobile phone, text messages, radio; agree set times to make contact
✓ A safe room (if possible)
  - Choose the safest spot in your home (see the "two walls" rule below),
    and put water, a torch, a first aid kit and cushions there in advance
✓ People with chronic illnesses: stock extra medicine in advance; see the chapter on stockpiling medicines for chronic illness
```

For how to make a family communication plan, see [Emergency Communication and Finding Family](../../../docs/02-灾难应对/06-应急通信与寻亲.md#灾前-家庭通信计划); for stockpiling medicines, see [Stockpiling Medicines for Chronic Illness](../../../docs/01-日常疾病和伤害/08-慢性病用药储备.md).

### During a conflict

**Protecting yourself**:
```
✓ Stay indoors (when it is safe)
✓ Keep away from windows
  - One of the worst injuries from a blast wave is flying glass; draw the curtains, and do not put beds or sofas by windows
✓ Basement or inner rooms
  - The "two walls" rule: keep at least two load-bearing walls between you and the outside,
    for example a stairwell, an inner corridor, or a bathroom without windows
✓ Store food and water
✓ Go out less
  - When you must go out, go with someone, and say where you are going and when you will be back
  - Do not go out during curfew hours
✓ Keep a low profile
  - Do not wear camouflage or other clothes that look military; do not carry binoculars or other things that could be misunderstood
✓ Avoid military targets
  - It is more dangerous near barracks, checkpoints, military convoys, command posts, communication facilities, bridges and fuel depots
✓ Do not photograph or upload pictures or locations of military facilities, troop movements or places that have been attacked
  - This information can be used to adjust attacks, and can also make you a suspect
```

**Under shelling or air attack**:
```
At once:
1. Find cover:
   - A basement
   - The ground floor of a solid building
   - Away from windows
   - If there is a civil air defence shelter, metro station or underpass nearby, go into the nearest one
2. Lie down
   - If there is no time to get into a building, lie flat where you are in a ditch, a hollow or behind a kerb
3. Protect your head
   - Cover your head and neck with your hands and your sides with your elbows; keep your mouth slightly open to reduce blast damage to the ears and lungs
4. Keep away from outer walls
5. Wait until it stops
   - Shelling often comes in wave after wave; a pause does not mean it is over.
     Wait for the all-clear or an official announcement before coming out
   - After coming out, do not go to look at craters or bombed buildings (risk of a second strike, collapse or fire)
```

### Air-raid sirens and civil air defence shelters

```
China's air-raid siren signals (sirens are tested every year in each area; listen out for them):
- Preliminary warning: sound 36 seconds, pause 24 seconds, repeated 3 times, 3 minutes in total
  → Get ready, take your emergency kit, and go to a civil air defence shelter as directed
- Air-raid warning: sound 6 seconds, pause 6 seconds, repeated 15 times, 3 minutes in total
  → Go at once into the nearest civil air defence shelter or underground space
- All clear: one continuous long sound for 3 minutes
  → Wait for instructions before leaving the shelter

Civil air defence shelters:
✓ Find out in normal times where the shelters near your home, workplace and school are (many underground car parks and
  underground shopping centres double as shelters, and their entrances have a standard sign); you can also ask your
  residents' committee or the local civil air defence office
✓ Once inside, follow the staff's directions and do not push; older people, children and pregnant women go in first
✓ Take water, food, everyday medicines, a torch and warm clothes
```

**Caught in a gunfight**:
```
At once:
1. Get down!
   - Standing up makes you the biggest target; drop flat the moment you hear gunfire, do not first look for where it is coming from
2. Find cover:
   - Walls, pillars
   - The engine side of a car
     (car doors, wooden doors, ordinary partition walls and bushes only hide you from view; they do not stop bullets)
   - Solid furniture
3. Crawl to safety
   - Crawl low, out of the straight line of windows and doorways
4. Do not stand up or run
   - Move quickly only when you are sure there is a safe way out and staying put is more dangerous
5. Protect your head, chest and abdomen
6. After the shooting stops, wait a while longer and make sure it is safe before getting up;
   do not take photos and do not go closer to look
```

**Checkpoints**:
```
✓ Stay calm
✓ Keep your hands visible
  - When driving, slow down early, turn on the interior light (at night), and keep both hands on the steering wheel
✓ Obey instructions
✓ Move slowly
  - Before reaching for your papers, say clearly "I am going to take my ID out of my pocket", and wait for permission
✓ Do not move suddenly
✓ Carry identity documents
✓ Do not take photos and do not argue; if you are refused passage, turn back, change route or wait
```

### Unexploded ordnance

```
In conflict areas, and for a long time after a conflict ends, unexploded shells, bombs and landmines kill and injure people;
many of the victims are children (who pick them up out of curiosity).

❌ Do not touch, kick or move anything that looks like ammunition or an unknown metal object
❌ Do not pick up "souvenirs", and do not collect scrap metal
✓ If you find one: stop → walk slowly back the way you came → put a clear marker nearby (do not go close to place it) → report it to the local police or army
✓ Walk only on hard-surfaced roads that people use; do not enter abandoned fields, woods or houses
✓ Do not go past warning signs, red markers or tripwires
✓ Tell children again and again: do not touch anything you do not recognise
```

### Evacuation

**When to go**:
```
Consider evacuating when:
- The authorities advise evacuation
- The conflict escalates
  - The front line is getting closer, and shelling is more and more frequent
- Supplies are running out
  - Water, power and medicines have been cut for some time, with no sign of them coming back
- There is a safe evacuation route
  - Humanitarian corridors, officially organised evacuation convoys

When there is no safe route, holding out in a solid shelter is often safer than risking a journey through a combat zone.
```

**Preparing to evacuate**:
```
✓ Monitor official information
✓ Contact your embassy or consulate
✓ Choose a safe route
✓ Avoid combat zones
✓ Take your emergency kit
  - Water, food, medicines (enough for the journey and for a while after arriving), documents, cash, phone and power bank, warm clothes
✓ Travel light
  - One backpack each; leave behind whatever you cannot carry; keep your hands as free as possible
✓ You can use a white flag or similar to show you are civilians (do not use the red cross emblem: it is protected by law and only for medical and rescue workers)
✓ Travel by day and avoid moving at night (at night you are more easily mistaken for a threat)
✓ Put a card on each child with their name, their parents' phone numbers and their blood group, in case you are separated
```

**Evacuating as a group**:
```
✓ Follow the crowd
  - But not blindly; watch for official directions and the state of the roads
✓ Help each other
✓ Protect the vulnerable
  - Put older people, children, pregnant women, the sick and injured, and disabled people in the middle of the group
✓ Stay in contact
  - Agree a meeting point for anyone who falls behind; count heads regularly
```

For finding each other after a separation, see [Emergency Communication and Finding Family](../../../docs/02-灾难应对/06-应急通信与寻亲.md#家人失散后怎样寻找).

## Chemical spills

Chemical plant accidents, overturned hazardous-chemical tankers, pipeline leaks and warehouse fires can all release toxic gases or liquids. For ordinary people only three things matter most: **leave upwind or crosswind; if there is no time to leave, close the doors and windows and shelter where you are; if anything gets on you, take off your clothes and rinse**.

### Recognising a chemical spill

**Signs**:
- Unusual smells
  - Pungent, rotten eggs, garlic, bleach and so on; but many toxic gases are **colourless and odourless**. Not smelling anything does not mean it is safe, and some gases numb the sense of smell at high concentrations
- Smoke or vapour
  - Coloured smoke from an unknown source, white mist drifting along the ground
- Dead animals and plants
  - Birds, fish or insects dying in large numbers; grass and plants suddenly turning yellow
- Irritated eyes and throat
- Difficulty breathing
- Dizziness and nausea
- Skin irritation
- Many people around you start to have watering eyes, cough or collapse at the same time — this is the clearest warning sign. **Do not go closer to help them**; get away first, then call for help

### What to do at once

**If you are outdoors**:
```
1. Work out the wind direction:
   - Watch smoke and flags
   - Feel the wind
   - See which way leaves, washing on the line and chimney smoke are blowing
   - Wet a finger and hold it up; the side that feels cool is the direction the wind is coming from

2. Leave upwind or crosswind:
   ✓ At right angles to the wind
     - If you are right in the smoke plume downwind, first walk sideways (at right angles to the wind) out of the plume,
       then turn upwind and move away
     ❌ Do not run with the wind: the toxic gas travels with you
   ✓ Get away from the spill area
     - The further the better; do not stop to watch or take photos
   ✓ Go to higher ground (some gases are heavier than air)
     - Avoid low places such as underpasses, basements, ditches and the spaces under bridges

3. If you are in the spill area:
   - Cover your mouth and nose with a wet cloth
     (this only reduces the harm from some irritant gases; it is not a gas mask, so do not stay because you have one)
   - Get out quickly
   - Avoid contact with the liquid or fumes
     - Do not step in or touch spilled liquids, powders or residues
   - Expose as little skin as possible; if you can cycle or drive, leave quickly, with the windows shut and the air conditioning on recirculation

4. Once you are clear:
   - Take off contaminated clothing (not over your head)
   - Rinse your body (clean water, at least 15 minutes)
   - Get medical help
   - Call 119/120 and report where the spill is, its colour and smell, and what you saw
```

**If you are indoors**:
```
If the spill is outside and you are inside:

1. Decide:
   - Is it safer indoors? Go in
   - Or is it safer to leave?
   (If the government tells you to evacuate, evacuate. If there is no announcement, the fumes have already reached your door,
    and going out would mean walking through the gas, sheltering where you are is usually safer)

2. If you stay indoors:
   ✓ Close doors and windows
   ✓ Turn off ventilation and air conditioning
     - Also extractor fans, cooker hoods and fresh-air systems; turn off gas cookers
   ✓ Block the gaps under doors with wet towels
     - Seal window gaps and vents with tape and plastic sheeting
   ✓ Move to an upper floor (some gases are heavy)
     - Choose the room with the fewest windows; note: this is the opposite of nuclear radiation, see the comparison below
   ✓ Keep water ready (wet towels to cover your nose)
   ✓ Follow official instructions
     - Keep your phone, power bank and radio with you, and keep listening
   ✓ If the smell indoors gets stronger and symptoms start, call 119/120, explain the situation and do as they say

3. If you need to evacuate:
   - Go upwind
   - Protect your airway with a wet cloth

4. After the all-clear:
   - Once you receive the official all-clear, open all doors and windows to air the place, then spend a while outside in fresh air
```

### Shelter in place or evacuate

| Situation | Chemical spill | Nuclear and radiation accident |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| Where to go indoors | **Upstairs**, a room with few windows (most toxic gases are heavier than air and collect in low places) | **The basement or the middle of the building**, away from outer walls and the roof (the thicker the walls and floors, the more radiation they block) |
| Seal the room? | Yes: close doors and windows, turn off air conditioning, seal gaps around doors and windows | Yes: close doors and windows, turn off ventilation |
| How long, usually | Mostly a few hours; wait for the all-clear | Be ready to stay at least 24 hours; follow official announcements |
| In common | Close doors and windows, turn off air conditioning, listen for official information, do not go out to look | Same as left |

### Specific chemicals

**Chlorine** (yellow-green, pungent):
```
- Heavier than air, sinks
- Evacuate upwind and to higher ground
- You can cover your mouth and nose with a towel soaked in baking-soda water; for skin contact, rinse with clean water only
- After breathing it in there may at first be only a cough and chest tightness, with serious breathing difficulty only
  hours later; anyone who breathed in a lot should see a doctor and be observed, even if they seem better for now
```

**Ammonia** (irritating, foul smell):
```
- Ammonia gas is lighter than air, but a liquid ammonia leak forms a cold, heavy white cloud that spreads along the ground
- Evacuate upwind or crosswind; do not hide in low places
- You can cover your mouth and nose with a towel soaked in clean water (or dilute vinegar water); for skin contact, rinse with clean water only
- Liquid ammonia splashed on the skin also causes frostbite; likewise rinse first with plenty of clean water, and do not rub
```

**Sulfuric acid (mist or liquid)**:
```
- Sulfuric acid mist or dilute acid on the skin: rinse at once with plenty of running water for at least 15 minutes
- Concentrated liquid acid: first blot it off quickly with a dry cloth (do not wipe or smear), then immediately rinse with plenty of running water
- A small amount of water on concentrated sulfuric acid gives off heat, so always use a large amount of water
```

**Unknown chemicals**:
```
- Do not touch
- Do not smell
- Keep away
- Call for help
- Note (from a distance) the hazard signs, numbers and colours on the tanker or storage tank, and tell 119
```

For toxic gas from mixing common household cleaners (toilet cleaner + "84" bleach and so on), see [Poisoning – Cleaning product poisoning](../../../docs/01-日常疾病和伤害/12-中毒.md#清洁剂中毒).

### Decontamination

**Protect yourself before helping others**: do not rush into fumes or a spill area to rescue someone; every year rescuers are poisoned along with the people they try to help. If the casualty can walk, guide them out with your directions; if they cannot, leave it to firefighters with protective equipment. When helping someone decontaminate, wear gloves (if you have none, put plastic bags over your hands), and do not touch contaminated clothing or skin directly.

**Skin contact**:
```
1. Take off contaminated clothing
   - The sooner the better: taking off outer clothing removes most (up to about 90%) of the contamination
   - Do not pull tops over the head; cut them off with scissors so they do not brush the face, eyes, mouth or nose
   - Take off jewellery and watches; glasses can be worn again after washing in soapy water; throw away contact lenses
   - Put the clothes in a plastic bag, tie it tightly, put that in a second bag, place it away from people,
     and hand it to rescuers; do not wash it yourself
2. Rinse with plenty of water for 15–20 minutes
   - Rinse with running water from the head down, then wash gently with soap and water; do not scrub hard
   - Skin that is already red, blistered or burning is a chemical burn: rinse for more than 30 minutes, and longer for alkalis
3. Do not use neutralisers (unless you are told to)
   - "Neutralising" with vinegar or baking soda gives off heat and makes the injury worse
4. Get medical help
5. Take information about the chemical
   - The packaging, the label or a photo of it
```

For treating chemical burns, see [Wound and Injury Care – Chemical burns](../../../docs/01-日常疾病和伤害/02-外伤处理.md#化学烧伤).

**Eye contact**:
```
1. Rinse at once (clean water)
   - Rinse first, then find help or make calls; every minute of delay can affect eyesight
2. Hold the eyelids open while rinsing
   - Injured eye lower, water running from the inner corner towards the outer corner
3. At least 15–20 minutes
   - If it still hurts after the full time, keep rinsing, and keep rinsing on the way to hospital if you can
4. Do not rub the eye
5. Get medical help
```

For how to rinse, see [Foreign Bodies – Chemicals in the eye](../../../docs/01-日常疾病和伤害/04-异物处理.md#化学品入眼紧急).

**Inhalation**:
```
1. Move to fresh air
   - Rescuers must not enter the toxic gas area themselves
2. Keep the airway open
   - Let the casualty sit up or half lie (breathing is easier); if they are not fully conscious but are breathing, put them in the recovery position on their side
   - Loosen the collar and belt; keep them warm, let them rest quietly, and do not let them walk about
3. If breathing stops, CPR
   - Chest compressions only; do not give mouth-to-mouth rescue breaths (the rescuer could be poisoned)
4. Get medical help
```

| Signs | What to do |
|------|------|
| Watering eyes, eye pain, sore throat, slight cough that eases soon after leaving | Get away from the scene, rinse, rest and watch; if it was chlorine or another irritant gas, tell a doctor and watch for any worsening that day |
| Persistent cough, chest tightness, shortness of breath, hoarse voice, dizziness and vomiting | See a doctor as soon as possible |
| Difficulty breathing, blue lips, coughing up pink frothy sputum, seizures, confusion or unconsciousness | Call 120 at once |

For the recovery position and CPR, see [Basic First Aid](../../../docs/01-日常疾病和伤害/00-基础急救知识.md#气道与复苏体位).

## Nuclear radiation

Radiation cannot be seen or smelled. In a nuclear or radiation accident, the main danger to the public is **radioactive dust (fallout)**: it settles on skin, clothes, the ground and food, and is breathed in or eaten. China's main public protection measures in a nuclear emergency are: **shelter, evacuation, taking iodine tablets (when told to), and controlling food and drinking water**.

### Sources of radiation

- Nuclear power plant accidents
- Nuclear weapons
- Leaks of radioactive material
  - Including lost medical or industrial radioactive sources; if you see a metal canister or metal part with the three-bladed radiation warning symbol, do not pick it up or take it home; keep away and call the police
- Dirty bombs
  - Ordinary explosives used to spread radioactive material; the blast itself usually does more harm than the radiation. Treat it first as a bomb attack, then leave the scene and decontaminate

### Principles of protection

**The three factors**:
```
1. Time: reduce exposure time
   - The shorter you stay in the contaminated area, the less radiation you receive
2. Distance: keep away from the source
   - Doubling the distance cuts the radiation you receive to about a quarter
3. Shielding: use materials to block it
   - The thicker the concrete, brick, stone or earth, the better; the middle of a building and basements shield best
   - Wooden huts and cars shield very little
```

### Responding to a nuclear accident

**Official warnings**:
```
✓ Follow instructions
  - Listen through television, radio, mobile phone text messages and community notices; do not believe or spread rumours
✓ Evacuate or shelter in place
  - The government sets zones by wind direction and distance, with different instructions for each; do what the notice for your zone says
✓ Get ready to evacuate
  - Put your emergency kit, documents and medicines by the door
```

**Sheltering in place**:
```
If the authorities advise you to stay indoors:

1. Go indoors:
   - Ideally a basement
   - A central room
   - Away from outer walls
   - The middle floors of a building are better than the top or ground floor (the top floor is close to fallout on the roof, the ground floor close to fallout on the ground)
   - People outdoors should get into the nearest solid building as quickly as possible; do not shelter in a car
   - Bring pets indoors too

2. Close doors and windows:
   - Seal them
   - Turn off ventilation
     (air conditioning, extractor fans, fresh-air systems, cooker hoods — turn them all off)

3. Take off outer clothing:
   - It may carry radioactive particles
   - Seal it in a plastic bag
   - Keep it away from living areas
   - Anyone coming in from outside takes off outer clothing and shoes at the door; removing outer clothing can remove most (up to about 90%) of radioactive contamination

4. Shower:
   - Wash gently
   - Do not scratch or graze the skin
   - Wash your hair with shampoo, not conditioner (conditioner makes radioactive particles stick to the hair)
   - If you cannot shower: wipe exposed skin and hair with a wet towel,
     blow your nose, wipe your eyelids and ears; seal used towels in a bag
   - Rinse wounds clean before dressing them

5. Supplies:
   - Food and water
   - A radio
   - Stay in contact

6. Wait:
   - Follow official announcements
   - Possibly for several days
   - Be ready to stay at least 24 hours; do not go out to look around without an announcement
```

**Evacuation**:
```
1. Evacuate by the designated route
   - Do not choose your own direction; do not go downwind
2. Take:
   - Emergency kit
   - Medicines
   - Identity documents
   - Phone, charger, a few changes of clothes
3. Close doors and windows
   - Turn off the main water, electricity and gas
4. When driving, keep the windows shut and the air conditioning on recirculation
   - People without a car go to the assembly point for transport as announced; help older people living alone and neighbours with limited mobility on the way
5. At the reception centre, have a radiation check and decontamination, and follow the staff's directions
```

### In a nuclear explosion

```
1. If you see an intense flash: do not look at it; lie down at once where you are and protect your head
   (the blast wave arrives seconds to tens of seconds after the flash; lying down stops you being thrown or hit by debris)
2. After the blast wave: get into the nearest solid building as fast as possible, the deeper the better (basement, middle of the building)
3. Radioactive fallout is most dangerous in the first few hours, and its intensity falls quickly over time;
   stay indoors for at least 24 hours unless officially told to evacuate
4. Once indoors, take off outer clothing and wash at once (as above)
```

**Iodine tablets**:
```
⚠️ Take them only when officially instructed!

What they do:
- Protect the thyroid
- Block uptake of radioactive iodine
  - Nuclear power plant accidents release radioactive iodine; once breathed in or eaten it concentrates in the thyroid and raises the risk of thyroid cancer;
    children are the most sensitive
  - Iodine tablets (potassium iodide) "fill up" the thyroid with stable iodine first so that it stops taking up radioactive iodine
  - Take them as soon as possible after the instruction; they work best taken before, or within a few hours after, exposure to radioactive iodine; taken too late they do little
  - Follow the instructions given out with them and the official requirements for the dose; doses differ for babies, children and pregnant women. Usually only one dose
    is needed; whether to take more is up to official announcements — do not increase the dose yourself

Note:
- They do not protect against other radiation
- They do not treat radiation
- There are contraindications
  - People allergic to iodine or with certain thyroid diseases should first ask a doctor or the medical staff on site
- Follow professional guidance
- Priority groups are babies and young children, children, pregnant women and breastfeeding women

❌ Do not eat iodised salt or drink tincture of iodine or povidone-iodine instead of iodine tablets:
   iodised salt contains too little iodine — you would get salt poisoning long before eating a protective amount;
   tincture of iodine and povidone-iodine are antiseptics for external use and are poisonous to drink
❌ Do not take them "as a precaution" when there has been no accident announcement
```

### Radiation protection

**Reduce exposure**:
```
✓ Keep away from radiation zones
✓ Stay indoors
✓ A basement is best
✓ Spend less time outside
  - When you must go out, wear a mask, long sleeves and long trousers; when you come back, take off outer clothing at the door and wash your hands and face
```

**Avoid taking it in**:
```
✓ Do not drink untreated water
  - Whether tap water is safe to drink depends on official announcements; water already in sealed containers before the accident is safe to drink
  - Do not drink rainwater from outdoors, or water from open-air tanks or ponds
  - Boiling and ordinary water filters do not remove radioactive material
✓ Do not eat local food (it may be contaminated)
  - Vegetables from gardens, fruit from trees, free-range poultry and livestock and their milk and eggs:
    do not eat any of them until they have been officially tested and declared safe
✓ Use sealed packaged food and bottled water
  - Food kept indoors, in cupboards or in the fridge is safe to eat; wipe the outside of tins and packets clean before opening
✓ Clean food
  - Food in undamaged packaging can be eaten after the outside has been wiped clean
✓ Do not panic-buy salt, iodine tablets or bottled water; collect supplies as officially arranged
```

**Long-term protection**:
```
✓ Monitor radiation levels
✓ Avoid heavily contaminated areas
✓ Regular health checks
✓ Counselling
  - The psychological stress of fear and uncertainty about radiation often affects more people than the radiation itself;
    if sleeplessness or anxiety does not ease, get help
```

**When to tell medical staff**: nausea, vomiting, weakness or reddened skin within a few hours of the accident may mean a large dose of radiation. Tell the medical staff on site at once or call 120, and say where you were at the time and how long you stayed there.

For surviving a nuclear disaster during long-term social breakdown, see [Survival After Civilisation Collapse – Nuclear disaster](../../../docs/03-野外生存/06-末日求生.md#核灾难); for the physics of radiation dose, see [Modern Physics](../../../docs/09-进阶科学与工程/06-现代物理.md).

## Terrorist attacks

### Types

- Bombings
- Shootings
- Vehicle ramming
- Knife and other violent attacks
- Chemical/biological/nuclear attacks
  - Treat a chemical attack as a [chemical spill](#chemical-spills) and a radiological attack as [nuclear radiation](#nuclear-radiation)

**Everyday precautions**: in crowded places such as stations, shopping centres and tourist sites, look for the emergency exits as you go in. Watch for people acting suspiciously, wearing heavy clothes in hot weather or repeatedly checking the security arrangements, and for unattended packages; if you see any, tell security staff or call 110.

### Bomb attacks

**At the scene**:
```
1. Lie down:
   - Face down
   - Hands protecting your head
   - Mouth open (reduces pressure injury)

2. Find cover:
   - Behind something solid
   - Away from windows and mirrors
   - Away from glass curtain walls, billboards and suspended ceilings that may collapse

3. Second explosion:
   - There may be one
   - Do not go back straight away
   - Leave quickly
   - Do not gather to watch; attackers sometimes deliberately target crowds and rescuers

4. Leave:
   - Get away from the scene
   - Go upwind
   - Help others
   - Do not touch suspicious objects
   - Use the stairs, not the lift; where there is smoke, keep low
   - Once clear, call 110/120 and say where it is; then let your family know you are safe (send a text; avoid calls that tie up the lines)
```

**Injuries at the scene**:
```
- Deal with severe bleeding first: press directly and hard on the wound with your hand or a thick cloth, and do not let go
- Consider a tourniquet only when severe bleeding from a limb cannot be controlled by pressure
- If a casualty cannot move or has neck or back pain, do not move them unless the scene is still dangerous
- Ringing in the ears and poor hearing after an explosion are common; but chest pain, shortness of breath or coughing blood may be lung damage from the blast wave —
  tell medical staff even if there are no visible injuries
- When there are many casualties, lead those who can walk away, and save your effort for those with severe bleeding or breathing difficulty
```

For how to stop bleeding, see [Basic First Aid – Stopping bleeding](../../../docs/01-日常疾病和伤害/00-基础急救知识.md#止血方法); for escaping fire and thick smoke, see [Fire – Escaping a fire](../../../docs/02-灾难应对/04-火灾.md#火场逃生).

### Shooting attacks

**The "Run, Hide, Tell" principle**: Chinese public security authorities and the counter-terrorism guidance of most countries follow this order — **run if you can; if you cannot run, hide; call the police as soon as you are safe**. Only when your life is directly threatened and there is no way out should you consider the last step, "fight back".

**1. Run**:
```
If there is an escape route:
✓ Run at once
  - Run away from the gunfire and the attacker; use corners, pillars and other cover, and do not stand still in the open or run in a straight line across it
✓ Leave your belongings
✓ Help others (if possible)
  - But do not waste time persuading others to come with you
✓ Stop others from entering the danger area
✓ Call the police
  - Once you are somewhere safe
✓ Once out, keep moving away; do not gather at the exits
```

**2. Hide**:
```
If you cannot run:
1. Hide behind solid cover
   - Only what stops bullets is cover (load-bearing walls, concrete pillars, heavy metal cabinets);
     ordinary doors, partitions and glass only hide you from view
2. Lock the door
3. Barricade the door
   - Wedge it with tables, cabinets or other heavy objects
4. Turn off the lights
5. Silence your phone
   - Turn off vibration too
6. Lie down, away from doors and windows
7. Stay quiet
8. If possible, contact the police (by text)
   - Many areas have a text-message police service (such as 12110); find out in normal times whether it works where you live.
     If you cannot text, call 110 and whisper your location; if you cannot speak, keep the line open
   - Give: location (floor, room), number of attackers, their appearance, direction, and whether anyone is hurt
```

**3. Tell**:
```
Once you are safe, call the police (110) at once and give:
- The exact location
- Number of attackers, their clothes and appearance, what they are carrying, which way they fled and in what vehicle
- Casualties
- Where you are now and how to find you

When the police arrive:
✓ Keep your hands empty, raised and fingers spread, so the police can see you are holding nothing
✓ Obey instructions; do not move suddenly; do not run towards the police or grab them
✓ The police must stop the attacker first and will not help the wounded first; this is normal
```

**Last resort: fight back**:
```
⚠️ Only when you cannot run, your hiding place has been found, and your life is directly threatened!

- Commit fully and give it everything; better if several people act together
- Use whatever is to hand (a chair, a fire extinguisher sprayed at the attacker's face, etc.) to stop them
- Aim: overpower them or escape
  - As soon as you get a chance to break free, run
```

### Vehicle ramming

```
Stay alert:
✓ Watch your surroundings
  - In pedestrian streets, squares and other crowded places, do not be completely absorbed in your phone
✓ Listen for unusual sounds
  - A roaring engine, sudden acceleration, screaming
✓ Watch for a vehicle speeding towards a crowd

What to do:
1. Run to the side
   - Do not run straight ahead in the direction the vehicle is travelling; run to either side, out of its path
2. Get behind obstacles:
   - Pillars, trees
   - Vehicles, buildings
   - Go into shops, stairwells or other places a vehicle cannot enter
3. If you cannot get behind anything, dodge to the side as best you can; do not stay where you are.
   After the vehicle stops the driver may get out and keep attacking; do not go near, keep moving away and call the police
```

### Knife and other violent attacks

```
✓ As with a shooting, follow "Run, Hide, Tell"
✓ When running, run towards crowded places with security staff, and shout loudly to warn others
✓ Do not try to grab the knife; the most important thing is to put distance between you and use chairs, backpacks or other objects as a barrier
✓ Bleeding from a stab wound: press hard on the wound; if the knife is still in the body, do not pull it out — secure it and get to hospital
```

For dealing with embedded objects, see [Wound and Injury Care](../../../docs/01-日常疾病和伤害/02-外伤处理.md).

### Hostage-taking

```
If you are taken hostage:

✓ Stay calm
✓ Obey instructions
✓ Do not resist (at first)
  - At the start the hostage-takers are most tense and most likely to hurt people
✓ Observe your surroundings (if possible)
  - Remember the number of hostage-takers, their appearance, accents and what they carry, and tell the police after your release
✓ Keep a low profile
✓ Do not look the hostage-takers in the eye
✓ Do not provoke them
  - Do not argue, cry or make a scene, or try to persuade them; ask calmly when you need water, the toilet or medicine
✓ Look after yourself: eat when you can, rest when you can; negotiations may go on for a long time
✓ If a rescue starts:
  - Drop to the floor at once
  - Put your hands on your head
  - Wait for instructions
  - Do not suddenly get up or run, and do not pick anything up (you could be mistaken for a hostage-taker)
```

### Suspicious objects

```
How to recognise them:
- Unattended packages
- Unusual shape
- Exposed wires or antennas
- Unusual sounds or smells
- Strange labels

What to do:
✓ Do not touch!
  - Do not open, move or shake it; do not pour water on it or cover it with anything
✓ Keep away
  - At least get somewhere behind a solid wall, away from glass
✓ Call the police
  - Make the call after you have moved away (do not use a phone or walkie-talkie near the object)
✓ Clear people from the area
✓ Wait for the professionals
```

## Stampedes and crowd crushes

Most deaths in a crowd crush are not from being "trampled": when a crowd is too dense, pressure from every direction stops people's **chests from expanding, and they suffocate**. Concerts, sports events, festivals, tourist sites, and the stairs and narrow passages of metro and railway stations are all high-risk places.

### Preventing crushes

```
✓ Avoid peak crowds where you can; with children or older people, try not to go to very crowded places
✓ When you arrive, find the emergency exits and evacuation routes, and agree a meeting point in case you get separated
✓ Wear well-fitting, non-slip flat shoes, not slippers or high heels; avoid clothes with long straps or ties
✓ Walk at the edge of the crowd, not in the middle
✓ Move with the flow, not against it; do not suddenly stop, turn round, crouch to tie a shoelace or pick something up
✓ Take special care on stairs, slopes, bridges and at the mouths of passages
✓ Do not egg people on or push; if you see pushing, shout "Stop pushing!"

⚠️ If any of the following happens, the crowd is already dangerous; move to the edge as soon as you can:
- You are pressed front and back and cannot control where you go
- The crowd surges back and forth like waves
- Breathing becomes difficult; you hear people calling for help or screaming
```

### Caught in a crowd

```
1. Stay on your feet — this is the most important rule
   - Feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent, centre of gravity low, stand firm
2. Protect your chest and keep space to breathe
   - Make a fist with one hand, grip that wrist with the other hand, and hold your arms flat across your chest with elbows out,
     using your arms to brace a little space in front of your chest
3. Go with the flow, moving diagonally towards the side
   - Do not force your way back, and do not stand and resist; each time the crowd loosens a little, edge a bit further towards the side
4. Keep away from dangerous edges
   - You can be crushed against corners, railings, glass shop windows and pillars; do not cling on to railings
5. Save your strength and shout less; when you need to call for help, put it into a few loud shouts
6. If you lose a shoe or drop something, do not bend down to pick it up
7. With a child: pick the child up and hold them against your chest, or let them ride on an adult's shoulders
```

### If you fall

```
1. Get up as fast as you can
   - Use the people around you to help; crouch first, then stand
2. If you cannot get up, take the protective position:
   - Lie on your side, curled up into a ball
   - Interlace your fingers and cover the back of your head and neck
   - Elbows forward to protect your temples
   - Pull your knees up to your chest as far as you can to protect your chest and abdomen
   - Do not lie on your back or face down (your chest and abdomen would be pressed directly)
3. As soon as the crowd loosens a little, get up at once
```

### If you see someone fall

```
✓ Stop at once and shout loudly "Someone has fallen! Stop pushing!" so the people behind know
✓ Several people nearby link arms, back to back, to form a ring that holds back the crowd behind, and pull the fallen person up
✓ If you are at the edge of the crowd or on higher ground: shout to the people behind and signal them to stop moving forward, and alert security staff and the police
```

### Helping after a crush

```
✓ Help others only once the crowd has dispersed and you are safe; call 120 and 110 first
✓ First help those who are unresponsive and not breathing normally: start CPR at once
✓ Responsive casualties: let them lie on their side or sit and rest; do not move anyone who may have a fracture unless necessary
✓ Anyone with chest tightness, difficulty breathing, coughing blood or abdominal pain after being crushed must go to hospital, even with no visible injury
```

For CPR, see [Basic First Aid – CPR](../../../docs/01-日常疾病和伤害/00-基础急救知识.md#心肺复苏cpr); for splinting fractures, see [Bone and Joint Injuries](../../../docs/01-日常疾病和伤害/03-骨骼损伤.md).

## Widespread power and water cuts

Grid failures, extreme weather and war can all cause large-scale, long-lasting power and water cuts. Only the key points are listed here; for details see the related chapters.

**Power cuts**:
```
✓ For lighting, LED torches, head torches and camping lanterns are best; if you light candles, do not leave them, and put them out before sleeping
✓ Never use a generator, charcoal fire or gas heater or cooker indoors, in a garage or on a balcony —
  carbon monoxide poisoning rises sharply during power cuts
✓ Open fridges and freezers as little as possible: a fridge usually stays cold for about 4 hours, a full freezer for about 2 days;
  after that, do not eat meat, cooked food or dairy products that have warmed up
✓ Plan ahead for keeping insulin and other refrigerated medicines cool
✓ People who use home medical equipment such as oxygen concentrators or ventilators should prepare a backup power supply in advance,
  and contact the hospital or community early if the power may be off for a long time
✓ Unplug appliances and leave just one light switched on, so you know when the power comes back,
  and so unstable voltage does not damage appliances or start a fire
✓ Save phone battery: turn down brightness, turn off features you are not using, text instead of calling
✓ Trapped in a lift: press the alarm bell, call the rescue number in the lift or 119;
  do not prise the doors open or try to climb out; stay calm and wait for rescue
```

**Water cuts**:
```
✓ When you are told the water will be cut off, store extra water in clean containers with lids; you can also fill the bath (for flushing the toilet)
✓ Allow about 2–3 L of drinking water per person per day, plus water for handwashing and cooking
✓ Boil water from unknown sources before drinking it
✓ Flush the toilet with stored non-drinking water; even when water is short, do not skip handwashing (before eating, after the toilet, before cooking)
✓ When the supply comes back, run the tap for a few minutes until the water is clear before using it
```

For emergency lighting, generators and fuel safety, see [Lighting and Energy](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/06-照明与能源.md#停电时的应急照明); for carbon monoxide poisoning, see [Home and Traffic Accidents](../../../docs/01-日常疾病和伤害/07-居家与交通意外.md#一氧化碳中毒); for electrical safety when the power comes back, see [Household Electrical Safety – Restoring power after a disaster](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/08-家庭用电安全.md#灾后恢复用电); for storing insulin, see [Stockpiling Medicines for Chronic Illness – During a power cut](../../../docs/01-日常疾病和伤害/08-慢性病用药储备.md#停电时); for how much water you need, storing and purifying it, see [Water Sources and Supply](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/10-水源工程与供水.md#需要多少水); for saving phone battery, see [Emergency Communication and Finding Family – Saving phone battery](../../../docs/02-灾难应对/06-应急通信与寻亲.md#手机省电).

## 📝 Summary

### Common principles for man-made disasters

```
1. Stay alert
2. Pay attention to warnings
3. React quickly
4. Protect yourself
5. Help others
6. Follow professional direction
```

### Responding to each kind of disaster

**War**:
- Keep a low profile
- Keep away from conflict zones
- Evacuate in time
- Do not touch unexploded ordnance

**Chemicals**:
- Work out the wind direction
- Evacuate upwind
- Seal yourself indoors (upstairs)
- Take off clothes and rinse; rinse eyes for 15–20 minutes

**Nuclear radiation**:
- Time, distance, shielding
- Shelter in place or evacuate
- Avoid taking it in
- Take iodine tablets only when officially told to

**Terrorist attacks**:
- Run, Hide, Tell
- Get away from the scene
- Leave quickly

**Crowd crushes**:
- Protect your chest, stay on your feet
- Move with the flow towards the side
- If you fall, curl up on your side and protect your head and neck

### Remember

```
✓ Prepare in advance
✓ Stay calm
✓ Decide quickly
✓ Do not panic
✓ Help each other
✓ Life comes first
```

### Psychological preparation

```
What man-made disasters are like:
- Sudden
- Uncertain
- Frightening

How to cope:
✓ Prepare yourself mentally
✓ Learn
✓ Practise with drills
✓ Keep hope
✓ Seek support
```

After such an event, sleeplessness, flashbacks and being constantly on edge are common reactions. For how to look after yourself and those around you, see [Mental Health](../../../docs/01-日常疾病和伤害/10-心理健康.md#经历危机后的正常反应).
