## ⚠️ Basic principles of disaster response

### Before a disaster (Prevention & Preparedness)

```
1. Know your risks
   - Disasters common in your area
   - Warning systems
   - Evacuation routes

2. Make a plan
   - Family emergency plan
   - Meeting places
   - Out-of-town contact

3. Prepare supplies
   - Emergency kit
   - Food and water
   - Backup copies of important documents

4. Learn skills
   - First aid knowledge
   - Self-rescue skills
   - Emergency drills
```

### During a disaster (Response)

```
1. Stay calm
2. Assess the situation
3. Make sure you are safe
4. Follow instructions
5. Evacuate in time
6. Help each other
```

### After a disaster (Recovery)

```
1. Check for injuries
2. Confirm it is safe
3. Contact your family
4. Assess the damage
5. Clean up and rebuild
6. Recover mentally
```

## 🎒 Family emergency kit

### Basic equipment

**Water and food** (at least 3 days' worth):
- Bottled water: about 4 L per person per day (about 2-3 L for drinking, the rest for cooking); store at least 3 days' worth
- Non-perishable food:
  - Tinned food
  - Compressed biscuits
  - Energy bars
  - Dried fruit, nuts
  - Baby food (if needed)

**Tools and supplies**:
- Battery-powered or wind-up radio
- Torches (several)
- Spare batteries
- Power bank
- Swiss Army knife / multi-tool
- Whistle
- Waterproof matches / lighter
- Candles
- Plastic sheeting, tape
- Rope
- Paper and pen

**Personal items**:
- First aid kit
- Prescription medicines (at least one week's supply, with a medication information card; see [Chronic medication stockpile](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/08-chronic-medication-stockpile.md))
- Glasses / contact lenses
- Personal hygiene items
- Wet wipes, disinfectant
- Rubbish bags
- Toilet paper

**Clothing and bedding**:
- Change of clothes
- Raincoat / waterproofs
- Sturdy shoes
- Hat, gloves
- Blanket / sleeping bag
- Emergency (foil) blanket

**Important documents** (sealed in a waterproof bag):
- Copies of ID card and passport
- Bank account details
- Insurance documents
- Medical records
- List of emergency contacts
- Family photos (for finding missing people)

**Cash**:
- Small notes and coins
- Cash machines may not work

**Special needs**:
- Babies: formula milk, nappies, bottles
- Older people: medicines, hearing-aid batteries
- Pets: food, water, lead, carrier

### In-car emergency kit

- Basic emergency kit items
- Jump leads
- Window breaker
- Warning triangle
- Fire extinguisher
- Map
- Blanket
- Shovel

## 🏠 Home safety checks

### Routine checks

**Structural safety**:
```
✓ Secure tall furniture (bookcases, wardrobes)
✓ Check the building structure (cracks, tilting)
✓ Install smoke alarms
✓ Keep a fire extinguisher
```

**Gas, water and electricity**:
```
✓ Know how to shut off the mains
✓ Check gas pipes
✓ Replace ageing wiring
✓ Keep emergency lighting
```

**Escape routes**:
```
✓ Plan at least 2 escape routes
✓ Keep passages clear
✓ Keep an escape rope (high floors)
✓ Practise regularly
```

## 👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Family emergency plan

### Making the plan

1. **Meeting places**:
   - Near home: a safe spot outside the house
   - In the neighbourhood: a school, park, etc.
   - Outside the neighbourhood: a relative's or friend's home, a designated place

2. **Out-of-town contact**:
   - Choose a relative or friend in another area
   - Everyone in the family memorises the number (and writes it on paper)
   - Report that you are safe to them during a disaster
   - See [Emergency communication and finding family](06-emergency-communication-and-family-reunification.md)

3. **Important information card**:
   - Everyone carries one
   - It contains:
     * Emergency contacts
     * Medical information
     * Blood group, allergies
     * Medicines taken

4. **Members with special needs**:
   - Older people
   - Children
   - Disabled people
   - Pets

### Drills

- Practise once every six months
- Include:
  - Emergency evacuation
  - Shutting off water, electricity and gas
  - Using the emergency kit
  - Contacting the out-of-town relative or friend

## 🆘 Distress signals

### Internationally recognised

**Sound signals**:
- Three short whistle blasts, pause, repeat
- Repeat at 1-minute intervals

**Visual signals**:
- SOS (... --- ...)
- Three fires (arranged in a triangle)
- Flashes from a signal mirror
- Waving brightly coloured clothing
- Large letters on the ground (HELP, SOS)

**Mobile phone**:
- Send your location
- Leave a message reporting the situation
- Save battery

## 🔔 Important reminders

### When disaster strikes

```
✓ Life first, property second
✓ Follow professional direction
✓ Do not go back without thinking
✓ Help others (as long as it is safe)
✓ Keep in contact
✓ Record and keep evidence
```

### Avoid rumours

```
✓ Follow official information
✓ Do not believe or spread rumours
✓ Check where information comes from
✓ Keep a level head
```

### Mental preparation

```
✓ Psychological trauma after a disaster is normal
✓ Seek professional psychological support
✓ Support each other as a family
✓ Take part in rebuilding the community
✓ Keep hope
```

How to help frightened family members and neighbours, how to look after children, and when professional help is needed: see [Mental health](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/10-mental-health.md).
