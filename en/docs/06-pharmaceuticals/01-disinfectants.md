# Making Disinfectants

> **The first line of defence against infection: the most practical and most important skill after a collapse**

## ⚠️ Read before use

```
1. If you can buy ready-made products (medical alcohol, povidone-iodine,
   "84" bleach (sodium hypochlorite), household bleach), use them; the
   home-made methods in this chapter are only for extreme situations when
   nothing can be bought and there is no stockpile
2. Home-made alcohol is for external use only. Never drink it, and never
   mix it into drinks (it may contain methanol)
3. Do not mix different disinfectants; above all, chlorine disinfectants
   must not meet acids or alcohol (see section 8.2)
4. Disinfectants only reduce the chance of infection; they cannot treat
   infection. If a wound gets redder, more swollen, hotter or more painful,
   oozes pus, or there is fever → see a doctor promptly, and call 120 if you
   can get through
5. All disinfectants are poisonous: label them, never keep them in drink
   bottles, and keep them out of children's reach
```

## ✅ Why are disinfectants the most important?

**After a collapse, the main cause of death is not hunger but infection!**

- Small wound infected → sepsis → death
- Dirty drinking water → cholera, typhoid → death
- Surgery without disinfection → infection → death

**Effective disinfection can:**
- Block 90% of routes of infection
- Help more wounds heal
- Make food keep longer
- Purify drinking water

**High success rate, low risk, immediately useful! Learn this first!**

## 1. Making alcohol (ethanol)

### 1.1 Why alcohol matters

**Uses:**
- Disinfecting skin (75% is best)
- Disinfecting instruments
- Disinfecting wounds (minor)
- Solvent (for making medicines)
- Fuel (spirit lamps)

**Effective concentrations:**
- 75% alcohol: the best concentration for killing bacteria
- 95% alcohol: too strong, and actually less effective (it coagulates protein, which blocks penetration)
- <60% alcohol: not effective enough

### 1.2 Making alcohol by fermentation

#### Principle:
```
Sugar + yeast → alcohol + carbon dioxide
C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂↑
```

#### Materials:

**Source of sugar (choose one):**
- Grain (rice, wheat, maize): starch → sugar → alcohol
- Fruit (grapes, apples): contains sugar directly
- Honey: high in sugar
- Sugar cane juice, sugar beet: high in sugar

**Yeast:**
- *Jiuqu* (traditional Chinese fermentation starter)
- Baker's yeast
- Wild yeast (on fruit skins; most on grape skins)

#### Steps (using rice as an example):

**Step 1: saccharification (starch → sugar)**
```
1. Wash 2 kg rice
2. Steam or boil until soft and mushy
3. Spread out to cool to 30°C
4. Mix in 100 g jiuqu (or sweet-rice-wine starter)
5. Keep warm and ferment for 24-48 hours
6. When it tastes sweet → saccharification has worked
```

**Step 2: fermentation (sugar → alcohol)**
```
1. Add 4-6 L water to the saccharified rice
2. Mix well and pour into a fermenting bucket
3. Cover (not airtight; leave a vent)
4. Keep at 20-30°C
5. Ferment for 7-14 days
   - Early: vigorous bubbling
   - Middle: gradually calms down
   - End: almost no bubbles
6. When it smells of wine → fermentation is complete
```

**Product: rice wine (5-12% alcohol)**

#### Steps (fruit wine, simpler):

```
1. Wash 10 kg grapes / apples and let them dry
2. Crush them (keep the skins; they carry wild yeast)
3. Add 2 kg sugar (to raise the alcohol content)
4. Put into a container, 70% full (leave room for fermentation)
5. Close the top (not fully airtight)
6. Ferment at room temperature for 10-20 days
7. Strain to get fruit wine (8-15% alcohol)
```

### 1.3 Purifying by distillation

**Weak alcohol (5-15%) → strong alcohol (75-90%)**

#### Simple still:

⚠️ Alcohol vapour is flammable, heavier than air, and creeps along the ground to flames: work outdoors or somewhere well ventilated, keep the collecting bottle away from fire, and have a wet cloth and sand or soil ready (see "Fire safety" in section 1.4).

Condenser: a copper pipe coiled in a bucket of cold water; if you have no copper pipe, use bamboo. Bamboo cannot be coiled, so it can only be a straight tube passing at a slant through a trough of cold water. Condensing works best when cold water comes in at the bottom of the bucket and the warmed water overflows at the top (hot water rises); without a water pipe, change the cold water often.

![Simple still: a large pot filled about 70% with rice wine or fruit wine, heated gently over charcoal, temperature kept at 78-85°C; the vapour passes through a pipe in the lid into a condenser coiled in a bucket of cold water, condenses into droplets and runs into a receiving bottle; discard the heads, collect the middle, stop collecting at the tail above 85°C](../../images/simple-still.svg)

*Figure: The fire is under the pot, and the vapour is led out through a pipe from a hole in the lid (see [Pharmacy basics – equipment you can make](00-pharmacy-basics.md#71-equipment-you-can-make)). For pipe materials see Q3 below; for flammable vapour and fire safety during distillation see [Pharmacy basics – ventilation](00-pharmacy-basics.md#62-ventilation) and "Fire safety" in section 1.4*

#### Procedure:

**1. Filling:**
- Fill a large pot with rice wine / fruit wine (70% full)

**2. Heating:**
- Heat slowly on a low fire
- Keep the temperature at 78-85°C (alcohol boils at 78°C)

**3. Distilling:**
```
Temperature control is the key:
- Start (heads): contains methanol, acetaldehyde, etc. Discard at least the
  first distillate (100 mL or more for every 10 L of starting liquid)
  ⚠️ Methanol is highly toxic and can cause blindness and death. Home-made
  alcohol is for external use only; never drink it!
- Middle: strong alcohol (collect it! a simple still reaches about 90% at most)
- Tail: water comes over (>85°C; stop collecting)

How to tell:
  - Dabbed on the hand, it evaporates quickly with a cool feeling → strong ✓
  - Does not evaporate easily, feels sticky → weak alcohol or water ✗
```

**4. Measuring the concentration:**
```
Method 1: alcohol meter (ideal)
  - Read the scale

Method 2: burning (very rough, unreliable)
  - Can only roughly tell high from low; cannot confirm whether it has
    reached 75%
  - Light a small cup of the alcohol
  - Burns away completely with no residue → about 95% or above
  - A little residue left → 70-90%
  - Goes out half-way → <60%

Method 3: weighing
  - Density of pure alcohol: 0.79 g/mL
  - Density of water: 1.0 g/mL
  - Weigh 100 mL and calculate
```

**5. Repeated distillation:**
- First: about 10% → about 30-40%
- Second: → about 60-70%
- Third: → about 85-90%

⚠️ The practical limit of a simple still is about 90%. Alcohol and water form an azeotrope, so even in theory ordinary distillation can reach only about 95.6%.

**Yield:**
- 10 L rice wine (10%) contains about 1 L pure alcohol → after three distillations about 0.7-0.9 L strong alcohol (about 90%)
- About 20-40% of the alcohol in the starting liquid is lost in the discarded heads, the tail left in the pot, and evaporation

### 1.4 Using alcohol

**Making 75% alcohol:**
```
Formula: diluting 95% alcohol to 75%
Take 95% alcohol: 79 mL
Add distilled water (or cooled boiled water): 21 mL
Mix well = 100 mL of 75% alcohol

Or, simplified:
about 4 parts alcohol : 1 part water
```

Check the concentration once it is mixed: if you have an alcohol meter, measure it. If you have no meter and can only estimate by burning or by feel, treat it as "concentration uncertain" and use it only for wiping objects; for skin and instruments, prefer other methods such as boiling and tincture of iodine.

**How to use:**
- Skin disinfection: wipe twice, allow about 3 minutes to act (until dry)
- Instrument disinfection: soak for 30 minutes
- Hand disinfection: rub until dry (more than 20 seconds)

**How to make it work:**
```
✓ Clean first, then disinfect: if hands or objects have mud, blood or grease
  on them, wash them with soap and water first; alcohol works much less well
  in contact with organic matter
✓ Use enough: wet the surface thoroughly and let it dry naturally; do not
  wipe or blow it dry straight away
✓ Skin before an injection or a needle prick: wipe in circles from the
  centre outwards, over an area about 5 cm across, and wait until it is dry
✓ Scissors, tweezers, needles: scrub them clean first, then submerge them
  fully in alcohol for 30 minutes; boil metal instruments that can be boiled
  instead (see section 11)
✗ Alcohol does not kill bacterial spores (e.g. Clostridium tetani, which
  causes tetanus) and works poorly against non-enveloped viruses such as
  norovirus; deep wounds that have had soil in them, and the vomit and
  faeces of people with diarrhoea, cannot be dealt with by alcohol alone
```

**What it can be used on:**

| Item | Can you use it? | Notes |
|------|----------|------|
| Intact skin, hands | ✓ | Main use |
| Skin around a wound | ✓ | Do not let it run into the wound |
| The wound itself | ✗ | Very painful and damages tissue; rinse with clean water and apply povidone-iodine |
| Small instruments, thermometers | ✓ | Soak or wipe repeatedly |
| Tables, door handles, mobile phones | ✓ | Wipe; do not spray over large areas |
| Drinking water, food | ✗ | Cannot be used to disinfect drinking water |
| Vomit, faeces | ✗ | Use a chlorine disinfectant (see section 6) |

**Do not use it:**
- ✗ On deep wounds (pain, tissue damage)
- ✗ On mucous membranes (eyes, inside the mouth)
- ✗ On large burns (absorbed, causing poisoning)
- ✗ For sponging the body to bring down a fever (it is absorbed through the skin; especially dangerous for babies and young children)
- ✗ On people allergic to alcohol (skin goes red or comes up in a rash after wiping)

**Fire safety:**
```
- Alcohol vapour is heavier than air, creeps along the ground to a source of
  fire, and ignites on contact with a flame or spark
- When disinfecting with alcohol, there must be no naked flames, cigarettes or
  stoves nearby; after rubbing alcohol on your hands, wait until fully dry
  before going near a fire
- Do not spray it into the air or over large areas in a closed room
- Burning alcohol has an almost invisible flame in daylight: smother a small
  fire with a wet cloth or a pot lid; do not blow on it, and do not throw
  water into a container holding alcohol
```
For dealing with fires see [Fire](../02-disaster-response/04-fire.md).

## 2. Making tincture of iodine

### 2.1 Advantages of tincture of iodine

**Compared with alcohol:**
- ✓ Kills a wider range of germs
- ✓ Works against bacteria, viruses, fungi and spores
- ✓ Only a small amount is needed
- ✗ Irritant
- ✗ Stains (brown)

### 2.2 Sources of iodine

**Method A: iodine from seaweed (coastal areas)**

**Materials:**
- Seaweed such as kelp or nori: 10 kg
- Plant ash: 5 kg

**Steps:**
```
1. Dry the seaweed in the sun
2. Smoulder it to ash at a low temperature (too fierce a fire makes the
   iodine escape as purple vapour and be lost)
3. Boil the ash in water
4. Filter and keep the clear liquid
5. Evaporate to concentrate it
6. Add a dilute acid (e.g. vinegar) to make it acidic (the ash liquid is
   alkaline, and iodine cannot come out of solution under alkaline conditions)
7. Add hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂)
   Reaction: 2I⁻ + H₂O₂ + 2H⁺ → I₂ + 2H₂O
8. A brownish-black precipitate of iodine forms; collect it by filtering
   (alcohol cannot be used to extract it: alcohol and water mix completely
   and do not separate into layers)
9. Purify by gentle heating to sublime it; the iodine vapour condenses on a
   cold surface into purplish-black crystals

Yield: 10 kg kelp → 3-5 g iodine
```

**Method B: underground brine (inland areas)**

- Some salt lakes and brine wells contain iodine
- Extraction is similar to the seaweed method

**Method C: buy / salvage (realistic)**

- Tincture of iodine from pharmacies
- Povidone-iodine
- Iodised salt (far too little iodine; not practical)

### 2.3 Making up tincture of iodine

**Standard tincture of iodine (2%):**

**Recipe:**
```
Iodine (I₂): 2 g
Potassium iodide (KI): 1.5 g
75% alcohol: to make up to 100 mL
```

**Steps:**
```
1. Dissolve 1.5 g potassium iodide in 5 mL water
2. Add 2 g iodine and stir until dissolved
   (potassium iodide helps the iodine dissolve)
3. Add 75% alcohol to a total volume of 100 mL
4. Mix thoroughly
5. Store in a dark bottle (iodine breaks down in light)
```

**Simplified version (without potassium iodide):**
```
2 g iodine + 100 mL 95% alcohol
  - Low solubility; some does not dissolve
  - Slightly less effective, but usable
```

### 2.4 Using tincture of iodine

**Disinfecting:**
- Apply to the area
- Wait 2-3 minutes
- Remove the iodine with 75% alcohol (decolourise)

**Iodine allergy test:**
- Apply to a small area
- Watch for 15 minutes
- Redness, swelling or itching → allergic; do not use

**What it can be used on:**

| Item | Can you use it? | Notes |
|------|----------|------|
| Intact skin (before an injection or incision) | ✓ | Wait 2-3 minutes after applying, then remove the iodine with 75% alcohol |
| Small grazes, small cuts | △ | Very irritating and clearly painful; use povidone-iodine if you have it; if you only have tincture of iodine, apply it around the wound |
| Deep wounds, large wounds, burns | ✗ | Damages tissue; over large areas too much iodine is absorbed |
| Mucous membranes (mouth, eyes, perineum) | ✗ | Strongly irritant |
| Instruments, objects | △ | Stains and corrodes some metals; usually use alcohol or boiling instead |
| Drinking water | ✗ | This book does not recommend disinfecting drinking water with tincture of iodine; boil it or use chlorine instead (see section 6.2) |

**Who should not use it, or use it sparingly:**
```
✗ People allergic to iodine
✗ Newborn and premature babies (iodine is absorbed through the skin and may
  affect the thyroid)
⚠️ Pregnant women, breastfeeding women, people with thyroid disease: only on
  small areas, for short periods
⚠️ Children under 2: use povidone-iodine instead
```

**Common mistakes:**
- ✗ Not removing the iodine after applying it, or bandaging or making wet compresses with tincture of iodine: the skin blisters and burns
- ✗ Using it together with merbromin ("red medicine", mercurochrome): forms mercuric iodide, which is very irritant and damages the skin
- ✗ Drinking tincture of iodine "to top up iodine" or to protect against radiation: it causes poisoning (see [Man-made disasters](../02-disaster-response/05-man-made-disasters.md))

**Storage and loss of strength:**
- Dark glass bottle, cap screwed tight, in a cool place
- If the cap is not tight, the alcohol evaporates, the iodine becomes more concentrated and more irritant; black particles at the bottom of the bottle mean it has become too strong, and it should no longer be put directly on skin
- A clearly paler colour (from dark brown to pale yellow) means iodine has been lost and it will not kill germs well enough; make up a new batch

## 3. Povidone-iodine

### 3.1 Povidone-iodine vs tincture of iodine

| Property | Povidone-iodine | Tincture of iodine |
|------|------|------|
| Irritation | Low | High |
| Pain | None | Marked |
| Staining | Light | Heavy |
| Stability | Good | Poorer (breaks down in light) |
| Difficulty of making | Complicated | Simple |

### 3.2 Making it yourself is not advised

A solution made by dissolving iodine in washing-up liquid or another surfactant is not the same as povidone-iodine: its germ-killing effect, irritancy and stability have not been tested.

**Alternatives:**
- When you need an iodine disinfectant, use 2% tincture of iodine (see the previous section) instead
- Stockpile ready-made povidone-iodine before a collapse (stored away from light and sealed)

### 3.3 Using ready-made povidone-iodine

Household povidone-iodine usually contains about 0.5% available iodine (5 g/L), but products differ. **Use it as the instructions on the bottle say.**

```
Skin and wounds:
1. First rinse the wound clean with clean water (see Wound and injury care)
2. Dip a cotton bud in the undiluted solution and apply in circles from the
   centre of the wound outwards, to about 2-3 cm beyond the wound
3. No need to remove the iodine; let it dry naturally. One application is
   enough; there is no need to build up thick layers
4. Apply again when changing the dressing; there is no need to apply it
   every few hours

Mucous membranes (mouth, perineum, etc.): use only products whose
instructions say they can be used on mucous membranes, diluted as instructed
```

| Item | Can you use it? | Notes |
|------|----------|------|
| Wounds, grazes, animal bites | ✓ | First choice after rinsing |
| Intact skin | ✓ | Wipe twice, allow 2-3 minutes to act |
| Eyes | ✗ | Rinse with clean water or saline |
| Large burns | ✗ | Too much iodine is absorbed over large areas; treat burns as in the Wound and injury care chapter |
| Drinking water | ✗ | Do not disinfect drinking water with povidone-iodine |

**Storage and loss of strength:** store away from light, sealed, in a cool place; once opened, use it up within the time given in the instructions if possible. If the colour changes from reddish-brown to pale yellow or colourless, the available iodine has broken down and it can no longer be used.

**Who should take care:** the same as for tincture of iodine (iodine allergy, newborns, pregnancy and breastfeeding, thyroid disease). Povidone-iodine is less irritant, but it still should not be used over large areas for long periods.

For the full steps of wound care see [Wound and injury care – disinfecting](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/02-wounds-and-injuries.md#3-disinfect).

## 4. Hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂)

### 4.1 Uses of hydrogen peroxide

**Advantages:**
- Broad-spectrum germ killer
- Breaks down into water and oxygen (no toxic residue)
- Cleans wounds (the bubbling carries out dirt)

**Disadvantages:**
- Unstable; breaks down easily
- Mildly damages tissue

### 4.2 Making hydrogen peroxide (difficult)

**⚠️ Warning: making it is dangerous; this is for information only!**

**Method A: electrolysis**
```
Electrolyse dilute sulphuric acid:
  - Anode: produces peroxysulphuric acid
  - Hydrolysis: gives hydrogen peroxide
  - Purify by distillation

Dangers: needs electricity and sulphuric acid; risk of explosion
```

**Method B: barium peroxide**
```
BaO₂ + H₂SO₄ → H₂O₂ + BaSO₄↓
  - Add dilute sulphuric acid to barium peroxide
  - Gives hydrogen peroxide and a precipitate of barium sulphate
  - Separate by filtering

Danger: barium compounds are highly toxic!
```

**Conclusion: after a collapse, hydrogen peroxide is extremely hard to make. Use alcohol and tincture of iodine first!**

### 4.3 Using hydrogen peroxide

**Medical concentration: 3%**

**Use:**
- Cleaning contaminated wounds
- Bubbling carries out dirt and dead tissue
- After using it, rinse again with saline
- Not for deep wounds (bubbles hinder healing)

**Principles of use:**
```
✓ Use it once only, when a wound is very dirty (lots of soil, scabs or
  foreign matter): pour it on, let it bubble for about 1 minute, then
  rinse clean with clean water or saline
✗ Do not use it every day to "disinfect": it also kills new tissue and makes
  the wound heal more slowly
✗ Not for deep, narrow puncture wounds or body cavities (gas getting into
  tissue is dangerous), the eyes, or inside the ear canal (if the eardrum
  is perforated)
✗ Never let industrial hydrogen peroxide (usually around 30%) touch the skin
  directly: the skin turns white at once and is burned. Use only products
  marked "3% medical"
```

**Storage and loss of strength:**
- In its original light-proof bottle, tightly closed, in a cool place; it breaks down faster once opened
- A bulging bottle means oxygen from breakdown; point the mouth away from you when opening it
- To tell whether it still works: pour a little on blood from a wound or on a cut raw potato; **hardly any bubbling** means it no longer works

**In the eye by accident:** rinse at once with plenty of clean water for at least 15 minutes, then see a doctor.

## 5. Potassium permanganate solution

### 5.1 Uses of potassium permanganate

**Properties:**
- Strong oxidising agent
- Kills germs, disinfects, removes smells
- Deep purple; easy to recognise

### 5.2 Sources of potassium permanganate

**Industrial-grade potassium permanganate:**
- May be sold in chemical supply shops
- Deep purple crystals

**Hard to make at home after a collapse, but it can be stockpiled in quantity (stable)**

### 5.3 Making up potassium permanganate solution

**Different concentrations for different uses:**

| Use | Concentration | Colour | How to make |
|------|------|------|------|
| Rinsing wounds, wet compresses, sitz baths | 0.02% (1:5000) | Pale purplish-red, transparent; you can see the bottom of the cup clearly through it | 0.2 g + 1 L water |
| Intact skin | 0.05% | Purplish-red | 0.5 g + 1 L water |
| Disinfecting fruit | 0.02% | Pale purplish-red | 0.2 g + 1 L water |
| Disinfecting drinking water | Not recommended | — | Boil it or use chlorine (see section 6.2) |

Without a balance, judge by colour: better too pale than too dark. If the solution is deep purple and you cannot see through it, it is too strong and will burn skin and wounds.

**Use:**
- Soak for 10-15 minutes
- Rinse with clean water

**Key points:**
```
✓ First dissolve the crystals completely in a little water, then add water
  to the amount needed; undissolved crystals stuck to the skin cause brown,
  corrosive burns
✓ Make it fresh each time: when the solution turns from purplish-red to
  brown, it no longer works
✓ Skin, nails and clothes are stained brown; this fades on its own in a few
  days and is not a burn
✗ Do not use in the eyes or inside the mouth
✗ Do not use for long periods or in large amounts on large wounds
```

**Do not:**
- ✗ Mix it with glycerol, alcohol or other reducing substances (explosion!)
- ✗ Use it too strong (damages skin)
- ✗ Take it internally: swallowing crystals or strong solution corrodes the mouth, gullet and stomach; deal with it at once as in [Poisoning](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/12-poisoning.md#swallowing-cleaning-products-or-disinfectant) and call 120

## 6. Bleaching powder (chlorine disinfectants)

### 6.1 Uses of bleaching powder

**Advantages:**
- Cheap and effective
- Suitable for disinfecting large areas
- Disinfecting drinking water

**Disadvantages:**
- Strongly irritant
- Corrosive
- Unstable (loses strength when damp)

### 6.2 Using bleaching powder

**Making a disinfectant solution:**
```
10 g bleaching powder + 1 L water = 1% disinfectant solution

Uses:
  - Disinfecting floors and walls
  - Disinfecting toilets
  - Disinfecting areas with an epidemic
```

Bleaching powder contains about 25-30% available chlorine (high-test bleaching powder about 60-70%; check the pack first), so the 1% bleaching powder solution above contains about 2500-3000 mg/L available chlorine. This is fairly strong and is only for toilets, epidemic areas and obviously contaminated places. For everyday wiping, dilute further as in the table below; the concentrations match those for ["84" bleach](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/15-infectious-disease-at-home.md#84-bleach-chlorine-based-disinfectant):

| Use | Target available chlorine | 1% bleaching powder solution : water | Contact time |
|------|-----------|-----------------|----------|
| Everyday surfaces, tableware, clothing | About 500 mg/L | 1 : 4 | 30 minutes, then rinse with clean water |
| Items used by a sick person, toilets, contaminated floors | About 1000 mg/L | 1 : 2 | 30 minutes |
| Toilets, epidemic areas, around cesspits | About 2500 mg/L | Undiluted | 30 minutes or more |

Making it up: mix the bleaching powder to a paste with a little water first, then add the rest of the water and stir well; let it settle and use the clear liquid on top (the lime sludge at the bottom clogs sprayers and leaves white marks on things).

**Disinfecting drinking water:**
```
1. Add about 2 mL of 1% bleaching powder solution per litre of clear water
   (double for cloudy water); about 5 mg/L available chlorine, the same as
   2 drops of "84" bleach per litre
2. Stir
3. Leave to stand for 30 minutes
4. If it smells slightly of chlorine, it is safe to drink (residual chlorine
   protects it)
```

```
- Let cloudy water settle and filter it first, then disinfect the clear
  water; if you really have to use cloudy water, double the chlorine
- If you cannot smell any chlorine after 30 minutes, the chlorine has been
  used up by dirt in the water; add another dose and wait another 30 minutes
- If the chlorine smell is very strong and the water hard to drink, too much
  was added; leave it uncovered for a few hours or mix it with boiled water
  that has no chlorine before drinking
- Chlorine does not kill a few parasites such as Cryptosporidium; water for
  making up baby formula and for sick people is best boiled
```
When using household bleach (about 5% available chlorine): **2 drops per litre of clear water, 4 drops for cloudy water; shake and wait 30 minutes**. For the chlorine doses for wells, cisterns and large stores of water, see [Water sources and supply – disinfection](../07-everyday-living-skills/10-water-supply.md#disinfection).

### 6.3 "84" bleach and household bleach

The active ingredient of both "84" bleach and household bleach is sodium hypochlorite, usually about 5% available chlorine (it varies by brand; check the label). They are ready-made products and are easier to stockpile than bleaching powder.

- **Mixing ratios**: everyday 1:100, contaminated 1:50, vomit and faeces about 1:9 to 1:4; see [Infectious disease at home – making household disinfectant](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/15-infectious-disease-at-home.md#making-household-disinfectant)
- **Mix with cold water and make it fresh**; use diluted solution the same day
- **Contact time**: about 30 minutes after wiping or soaking, then wipe or rinse with clean water
- **Drinking water**: use only plain products with no perfume and no detergent; scented or "kitchen degreasing" types must not be used for drinking water

### 6.4 What chlorine disinfectants can be used on

| Item | Can you use it? | Notes |
|------|----------|------|
| Surfaces, floors, toilets | ✓ | Main use |
| Tableware, white clothing | ✓ | Soak in 500 mg/L for 30 minutes, rinse with clean water |
| Dark, wool and silk clothing | ✗ | Fades and damages them |
| Metal instruments | ✗ | Corrodes and rusts them; use alcohol or boiling |
| Vomit, faeces | ✓ | Cover with a strong solution for 30 minutes |
| Drinking water | ✓ | Only in very small doses (see section 6.2) |
| Skin, wounds, mucous membranes | ✗ | Irritates and burns; if it gets on your hands, rinse with clean water |

**Storage and loss of strength:**
```
- Bleaching powder: sealed, dry, away from light, cool; when damp it cakes
  and the chlorine smell fades, and the available chlorine gradually falls;
  once opened, use it up within a few months if possible
- "84" bleach, household bleach: in the original bottle away from light, cap
  tight; usually keeps about 1 year, and loses strength faster if stored in
  summer heat; do not use for drinking water if expired or if you cannot
  smell chlorine
- Keep all of them away from acidic products (vinegar, toilet cleaner) and
  flammable things, and not in the same cupboard as food
```

## 7. Normal saline

### 7.1 Uses

**Not a disinfectant, but extremely important:**
- Rinsing wounds (isotonic; does not damage tissue)
- Rinsing eyes
- Nose drops, nasal rinsing

⚠️ **Never give home-made saline into a vein or by injection**: it is not sterile and has not been tested for pyrogens; put into a blood vessel it can cause high fever, shock and sepsis. Intravenous fluids may only be given by medical staff using proper ready-made infusions.

⚠️ **Do not drink normal saline to replace fluids lost through diarrhoea and vomiting**: it contains no sugar or potassium, rehydrates less well than oral rehydration salts, and drinking a lot of it makes thirst worse. Use oral rehydration salts (ORS); for how to make them up see [Common illnesses – rehydration](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/01-common-illnesses.md#1-fluids-most-important).

### 7.2 Making it up

**Standard normal saline (0.9%):**
```
Table salt: 9 g (about 1.5 level teaspoons)
Distilled water or cooled boiled water: 1000 mL
Mix well
```

**Boil it to disinfect before use!**

```
- Bring the water to the boil first, dissolve the salt, then boil for a few
  more minutes; cover and let it cool to about body temperature before use
- Keep it in a boiled container with a lid; throw it away and make a new
  batch after 24 hours
- Use clean table salt, iodised or not; do not use coarse salt or pickling
  salt (too many impurities)
- When rinsing eyes and wounds, water close to body temperature is most
  comfortable; do not use it too hot or too cold
- Do not add too much salt: too salty stings the wound and damages tissue;
  saltier is not "more disinfecting"
```

## 8. Comparing disinfectants

| Disinfectant | Germ-killing effect | Irritation | Difficulty of making | Stability | Recommendation |
|--------|----------|--------|----------|--------|--------|
| 75% alcohol | ★★★★ | ★ | ★★ | ★★★ | ★★★★★ |
| 2% tincture of iodine | ★★★★★ | ★★★ | ★★★ | ★★ | ★★★★ |
| Povidone-iodine | ★★★★★ | ★ | ★ (buy) | ★★★ | ★★★★ |
| 3% hydrogen peroxide | ★★★ | ★★ | ★★★★★ | ★ | ★★ |
| Potassium permanganate | ★★★★ | ★★ | ★ (buy) | ★★★★ | ★★★ |
| Bleaching powder | ★★★★ | ★★★★ | ★ (buy) | ★★ | ★★★ |

**Recommended combination:**
1. **75% alcohol** - first choice; essential!
2. **Tincture of iodine** - powerful disinfection
3. **Normal saline** - for rinsing

### 8.1 Choosing a disinfection method by item

| Item | First choice | Can use | Do not use |
|------|------|--------|--------|
| Hands | Soap and running water for at least 20 seconds | 75% alcohol rubbed in until dry | Chlorine disinfectant |
| Wounds | Rinse with clean water or saline, then apply povidone-iodine | 3% hydrogen peroxide once if very dirty; 0.02% potassium permanganate | Alcohol or tincture of iodine inside the wound; chlorine disinfectant |
| Intact skin (before an injection or incision) | Povidone-iodine, or tincture of iodine then alcohol to remove it | 75% alcohol | Chlorine disinfectant |
| Metal instruments | Boil for 15 minutes | Soak in 75% alcohol for 30 minutes | Chlorine disinfectant (corrosive) |
| Tableware, towels | Boil for 15 minutes | Soak in 500 mg/L chlorine for 30 minutes | — |
| Tables, door handles | Wipe with 500 mg/L chlorine | Wipe with 75% alcohol (small areas) | — |
| Vomit, faeces | Cover with 5000-10000 mg/L chlorine for 30 minutes | Cover with quicklime | Alcohol |
| Drinking water | Boil | Chlorine (2 drops / 4 drops) | Alcohol, povidone-iodine, potassium permanganate |

### 8.2 Combinations that must not be mixed

```
❌ "84" bleach / household bleach / bleaching powder + toilet cleaner, vinegar
   or other acidic cleaners → releases chlorine gas; can be fatal
❌ "84" bleach + cleaners containing ammonia (some glass cleaners), or places
   where a lot of urine has collected → produces toxic chloramines
❌ "84" bleach + alcohol → produces chloroform and other harmful substances,
   and both stop working
❌ "84" bleach + hydrogen peroxide → they break each other down and both stop
   disinfecting
❌ "84" bleach + washing powder, washing-up liquid → most reduce its
   disinfecting effect
❌ Potassium permanganate + glycerol, alcohol, sugar, etc. → may catch fire
   or explode
❌ Tincture of iodine + merbromin ("red medicine") → forms mercuric iodide,
   which burns the skin
❌ Povidone-iodine + hydrogen peroxide applied together → they use each
   other up and work less well (after rinsing with hydrogen peroxide, rinse
   with clean water first)

Principle: use only one at a time; before changing to another, rinse or wipe
     clean with clean water; never pour them into the same bucket or onto
     the same cloth
```

If you mix them by accident and smell a sharp smell, or start coughing or feel tight in the chest, leave at once and open the windows; see [Poisoning – cleaning product poisoning](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/12-poisoning.md#cleaning-product-poisoning).

## 9. Stockpiling advice

### 9.1 Disinfectant stockpile list

**Minimum stockpile (family of 4, 3 months):**
- [ ] 95% alcohol: 5 L (can be diluted to about 6.3 L of 75%)
- [ ] Iodine: 20 g (makes 1 L of 2% tincture of iodine)
- [ ] Potassium permanganate: 500 g (makes about 2500 L of 0.02% solution)
- [ ] Bleaching powder: 5 kg
- [ ] Table salt: 10 kg (normal saline)

**Ideal stockpile (1 year):**
- [ ] 95% alcohol: 20 L
- [ ] Iodine: 100 g
- [ ] Potassium permanganate: 2 kg
- [ ] Bleaching powder: 20 kg

### 9.2 Storage points

**Alcohol:**
- Sealed (it evaporates!)
- Away from fire
- In a cool place

**Iodine:**
- Dark bottle (away from light)
- Sealed (it sublimes)
- Dry

**Potassium permanganate:**
- Dry (breaks down when damp)
- Away from light
- Away from organic matter (to prevent explosion)

**For all of them:** label every bottle with the name, concentration and date made up; never keep them in drink bottles or cups; lock them away or keep them high up out of children's reach; do not store them with food or medicines.

### 9.3 Shelf life and signs of loss of strength

| Disinfectant | Sealed, original packaging | After opening or making up | Signs it no longer works |
|--------|----------|--------------|----------|
| 95% / 75% alcohol | About 2 years | Keep the cap tight; the concentration falls as it evaporates; change the alcohol in a cotton-ball jar every week | Smell clearly weaker, no cool feeling on the hand |
| 2% tincture of iodine | About 2 years | In a dark bottle, cap tight; use within a few months | Paler colour; or black particles at the bottom of the bottle (too strong) |
| Povidone-iodine | As on the pack | As in the instructions | Reddish-brown fades to pale yellow or colourless |
| 3% hydrogen peroxide | As on the pack, usually 1-2 years | Breaks down faster once opened | Hardly bubbles on blood or raw potato |
| Potassium permanganate crystals | Keeps for many years if dry and away from light | Make solution fresh each time | Solution turns brown |
| Bleaching powder | About 1 year | Use diluted solution the same day | Caking, chlorine smell weaker |
| "84" bleach, household bleach | About 1 year | Use diluted solution the same day | No chlorine smell |
| Home-made normal saline | — | Use within 24 hours | Cloudy, sediment or odd smell |

The shelf lives of alcohol and tincture of iodine match [Pharmacy basics – storing medicines](00-pharmacy-basics.md#5-storing-medicines).

### 9.4 Toxicity, swallowing and splashes in the eye

No disinfectant may be taken by mouth. In the following situations, give first aid as in the table below, and at the same time call 120 or see a doctor as soon as possible, taking the bottle or label with you:

| Situation | Do at once | Do not |
|------|--------|--------|
| "84" bleach, bleaching powder, potassium permanganate or hydrogen peroxide swallowed | Rinse the mouth with clean water and spit it out; call 120 | Do not make them vomit; do not drink vinegar or soda water to "neutralise" it |
| Tincture of iodine or povidone-iodine swallowed | Rinse the mouth; call 120 | Do not make them vomit |
| Alcohol swallowed (especially home-made alcohol) | Treat as alcohol poisoning and call 120; it may contain methanol, so see a doctor even if they seem fine at the time | Do not let the person go to sleep alone |
| Splashed in the eye | Rinse at once with plenty of clean water for at least 15 minutes, blinking while rinsing, then see a doctor | Do not rub the eye |
| Splashed on the skin | Take off wet clothing, rinse with clean water | — |
| Chlorine gas breathed in (after mixing products) | Leave the area for somewhere well ventilated | If there is coughing, chest tightness or shortness of breath, do not assume "it will pass if I rest" |

```
Danger signs after a child swallows something (go to hospital at once):
- Drooling, difficulty swallowing, crying and refusing food (the mouth and
  gullet may be corroded)
- Vomiting, vomiting blood, abdominal pain
- Fast breathing, coughing that will not stop
- Drowsy, cannot be woken
```

For detailed treatment see [Poisoning – swallowing cleaning products and disinfectants](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/12-poisoning.md#swallowing-cleaning-products-or-disinfectant), [Poisoning – alcohol poisoning](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/12-poisoning.md#alcohol-poisoning) and [Poisoning – children swallowing things](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/12-poisoning.md#children-swallowing-things).

## 10. Frequently asked questions

### Q1: Can Chinese spirits (baijiu) be used to disinfect?

**A: It depends on the strength**
- Baijiu of 65%vol or more: just about usable in an emergency
- Baijiu of 56%vol: very poor
- Below 50%vol: basically useless

**Better to redistil it up to 75%!**

### Q2: What if I have no jiuqu (fermentation starter)?

**A: Wild fermentation**
- Grape skins carry natural yeast on the surface
- Just crush and ferment them
- Or take yeast from the lees of the previous batch

### Q3: What material should the still's pipe be made of?

**A: In order of preference**
1. Copper pipe (best; conducts heat well, non-toxic)
2. Stainless steel pipe
3. Bamboo (with the nodes knocked through)
4. Ceramic pipe
5. ✗ Plastic pipe (toxic substances leach out!)
6. ✗ Lead pipe (highly toxic!)

### Q4: How can I tell the alcohol strength?

**A: Quick checks**
- Lit, it burns steadily with a blue flame → strong (>60%)
- The flame flickers and soon goes out → weak (<40%)
- Dabbed on the hand, it evaporates quickly and feels cool → strong
- Feels sticky → weak

## 11. Emergency alternatives

**When you have no disinfectants at all:**

### Physical disinfection:

**Boiling:**
- Instruments: boil for 15 minutes
- Gauze: boil for 30 minutes
- Kills most pathogens

**Dry heat:**
- 160°C, 2 hours
- Metal and glass items

**Sunlight:**
- Ultraviolet light kills germs
- Expose for 6-8 hours
- Fairly weak effect

### Natural disinfection:

**Salt water (strong):**
- 20% salt water inhibits bacteria
- Do not use it to rinse wounds: too salty, it stings and damages tissue; rinse wounds with clean water or 0.9% normal saline
- Can be used to soak cleaning cloths and to preserve food by salting

**Vinegar:**
- Diluted vinegar (5%)
- Some antibacterial effect
- Cannot replace proper disinfectants

**Tea:**
- Strong tea
- Tea polyphenols inhibit bacteria
- Very weak effect

**Garlic juice:**
- Crush garlic and squeeze out the juice
- ✗ Not advised on wounds: it is strongly irritant, and left on the skin for a while it causes chemical burns and blisters
- Irritant

**Honey:**
- Apply to wounds
- Antibacterial, helps healing
- Not for deep wounds
- Use only on shallow wounds that have already been rinsed clean: spread a thin layer, cover with clean gauze, and at each daily dressing change rinse off the old honey with clean water before applying more
- If the redness spreads, pus appears or there is fever → stop and see a doctor

```
How to judge:
All the natural methods above are only "better than nothing" and cannot
replace rinsing and proper disinfectants.
The most important thing in wound care is always rinsing with plenty of
clean water;
once there are signs of infection, no home remedy can replace antibacterial
medicines and a doctor's treatment.
```
For signs of infection and when to see a doctor, see [Wound and injury care – signs of infection](../01-first-aid-and-family-health/02-wounds-and-injuries.md#-signs-of-infection-see-a-doctor-immediately).
