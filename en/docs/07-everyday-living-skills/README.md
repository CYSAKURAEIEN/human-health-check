# Everyday Living Skills

## 📋 Overview

This part covers the most basic skills of long-term living, and the ones most easily overlooked: how to store food, how to eat so that you do not fall ill, how to deal with excreta and rubbish, how to make soap, how to build a house that is safe to winter in, how to get light and energy when the power and supplies are cut off, how to mend torn clothes, how to tan hides and furs, how to use electricity safely, how to repair broken tables, chairs and farm tools, how to find and supply water, how to fire pottery and make paper, and how a community educates the next generation, keeps accounts, shares things out and divides the work; and also the jobs that come round every year: turning the harvested grain into rice, flour and oil, making salt, burning lime, twisting rope and tying knots, mending roads and building carts and boats.

These skills are useful in ordinary times, and they become critical when the water and power are off, supplies are short, or you must be self-sufficient for a long time. In past disasters and famines, large numbers of deaths came from deficiency diseases and from infections spread by the faecal-oral route, not from the disaster itself.

## ⚠️ The most important points

### Food safety

- ❌ **Do not make fermented rice-flour or maize-flour foods yourself** (*suantangzi*, *chouchazi*, *diaojiangba* and similar): bongkrekic acid poisoning has no specific antidote, the death rate is very high, and boiling does not destroy it
- ❌ **Without a pressure cooker, do not can vegetables or meat yourself**: boiling water does not kill botulinum spores
- ❌ **Throw away mouldy or bitter grain and nuts, the whole batch**: heating does not destroy aflatoxin
- ✓ Pickle vegetables for at least 20 days before eating them

### Nutrition

- Stored staple food only solves "hunger"; you also need beans and vegetables (including bean sprouts and wild greens)
- In winter, when vegetables are short, sprout beans for vitamin C
- Keep eating iodised salt

### Sanitation

- Toilets at least 30 m from water sources, and downhill of them
- There must be somewhere to wash hands next to the toilet
- Never put fresh excreta on vegetables

### Housing

- In earthquake-prone areas, adobe and rammed-earth houses must have earthquake-resistant measures (single storey, light roof, ring beam on top of the walls, ties at the corners); otherwise they collapse very easily, all at once
- Check the *kang*, stoves and chimneys for cracks and clean out the ash every year before winter; do not close the chimney damper fully before going to sleep; fit a carbon monoxide alarm

### Energy

- Never use petrol or alcohol in an oil lamp; in a power cut, use battery lamps for light first
- Never use charcoal or a generator indoors; never back-feed a generator into the house wiring
- **Never go down into a biogas digester**; if someone has collapsed inside, do not go straight down to rescue them

### Hides and furs

- Wear gloves when skinning and tanning; **never handle the hides of animals that died of disease or of unknown causes** (anthrax, brucellosis, tularaemia)

### Water supply

- Toilets and animal pens at least 30 m from water sources, and downhill of them; a well must have a raised platform and a cover
- **Before going down a well, lower a lamp to test it and blow air in to ventilate it**; if someone has collapsed down a well, do not go straight down to rescue them
- Let muddy water settle and filter it first, then boil it or disinfect it with chlorine

### Electricity

- Press the test button on the residual-current device once a month; never remove or bypass it
- Do not plug one power strip into another or plug high-power appliances into one; do not bring electric bikes into stairwells or homes to charge them
- Do not switch on appliances or wiring that have been flooded until an electrician has checked them

### Tools

- Stop using an axe or hammer at once and repair it if the head works loose; push blades away from you and clamp the work firmly

### Pottery and glass

- Do not fire kilns or furnaces indoors (carbon monoxide); let unfired pots dry thoroughly in the shade, and start firing with a small, slow fire
- **Never use lead glazes on vessels for food**
- Glass must be annealed after shaping

### Grain, oil, salt and lime

- **Throw away mouldy grain and peanuts, the whole batch, and do not press oil from them**; crude home-pressed cottonseed oil is not edible, and tung oil is poisonous
- Flour dust explodes on contact with a naked flame
- Never eat industrial salt (sodium nitrite), de-icing salt or salt of unknown origin; home-made salt contains no iodine
- Quicklime gives off a lot of heat with water; if it gets into the eyes, brush it out first, then rinse with water; let lime putty mature before using it

### Rope and transport

- Use the reef knot only for tying bundles, never for joining load-bearing ropes; do not stand in line with a rope under load
- On a boat, wear a life jacket or carry a float, do not overload, and do not take boats out or cross bridges in floods or strong wind

### Community organisation

- Several people must know each key skill (delivering babies, treating wounds, purifying water)
- Keep seed grain separately and never eat it; start rationing early
- At least two people present whenever the store is opened or goods handed out, and the accounts open to all
