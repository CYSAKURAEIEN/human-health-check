# Metallurgy

> **Smelting metal and making tools from scratch after a collapse**

## ⚠️ Important warning

**Metallurgical work is extremely dangerous!**

- 🔥 High-temperature work easily causes severe burns
- ☠️ Metal fumes are toxic; good ventilation is essential
- 💥 Molten metal explodes on contact with water
- ⚡ Professional protective gear is required
- 🏭 It causes serious pollution; work well away from water sources and homes

**Do not attempt this outside an emergency! This guide is only for extreme situations such as the collapse of civilisation and of the industrial system.**

### [01 Iron smelting](../../../docs/05-冶金技术/01-铁的冶炼.md)

The whole process from iron ore to usable iron objects.

**Main content:**
- 🪨 Identifying iron ore (magnetite, haematite, limonite)
- 🔥 Basic principles of ironmaking (reduction)
- ⚒️ From ore to pig iron
- 🛠️ From pig iron to wrought iron
- ⚡ Steelmaking (carburising)

**Key points:**
- Iron ore + charcoal → sponge iron (bloomery process, about 1200°C) or pig iron (liquid iron tapped at about 1300-1400°C)
- Pig iron is high in carbon (brittle) and must be refined into wrought iron
- Carburised wrought iron → steel

**Time cost:**
- Building the furnace: 3-7 days
- One iron smelt: 8-12 hours
- Yield: 1-5 kg of iron per smelt

### [02 Copper smelting](../../../docs/05-冶金技术/02-铜的冶炼.md)

How to smelt copper and make bronze.

**Main content:**
- 🟢 Identifying copper ore (malachite, chalcocite)
- 🔥 Smelting copper (melting point 1085°C, easier than iron)
- 🛡️ Making bronze (copper + tin)
- 🔨 Making brass (copper + zinc)
- 📿 Simple casting

**Key points:**
- Oxide copper ores (malachite, azurite) are the easiest to smelt; sulphide ores are hard and give off sulphur dioxide when roasted
- ☠️ Do not use ores containing arsenic (arsenopyrite, realgar and others): the arsenic fumes from smelting are highly toxic
- Remelting scrap copper comes first; it is much easier than smelting from ore
- Bronze (90% copper + 10% tin) is hard and is used for tools, vessels and bushings

**Advantages:**
- ✓ Low melting point; an ordinary furnace will do
- ✓ Easy to cast into shape
- ✓ Resists corrosion

### [04 Metalworking techniques](../../../docs/05-冶金技术/04-金属加工技术.md)

Treatments that improve the properties of metal.

**Main content:**
- 🌡️ Heat treatment
  - Quenching (rapid cooling to harden)
  - Tempering (reducing brittleness)
  - Annealing (softening for easier working)
  - Normalising (refining the grain)
- 🛡️ Surface treatment (rust prevention, carburising)

**Key points:**
- Quenching: heat to red, then cool fast in water
- Tempering: after quenching, reheat to about 220-300°C and judge by the temper colour for each tool (kitchen knife golden yellow, axe and sickle brown, hammer face purple)
- Correct heat treatment greatly increases hardness while keeping toughness; done wrong, the piece cracks or is too brittle

The most realistic source of metal after civilisation collapses: the ruins.

**Main content:**

- 🚗 Good steel in the ruins: what car leaf springs, files, bearings and rails are each good for

- ⚠️ Dangers of dismantling: sealed parts, galvanised parts, magnesium alloys, batteries

## 🎯 Learning path

### Stage 1: Preparation (1-2 weeks)
1. Learn the basics
2. Build a smelting furnace
3. Make charcoal
4. Identify and collect ore

### Stage 2: Salvage and first smelting (1-2 months)
1. Salvage scrap metal; learn to identify and sort it
2. Practise melting and sand casting with salvaged aluminium
3. Try smelting copper (fairly easy)
4. Practise forging and riveting with salvaged steel

### Stage 3: Advanced smelting (3-6 months)
1. Master iron smelting
2. Learn forging
3. Make iron tools
4. Master heat treatment

### Stage 4: Mastery (6 months+)
1. Steelmaking
2. Welding, advanced casting, fitting and machining
3. Sheet and wire (sheet-iron goods, copper wire)
4. Making complex tools and large-scale production

## ⚠️ Key safety points

### 🔥 Fire prevention
- Keep at least 10 m from anything flammable
- Have sand and water ready to put out fires
- No buildings downwind
- Night work needs a watchperson

### 💧 Preventing explosions
- **Molten metal must never touch water!**
- All tools must be completely dry
- Stop work when it rains
- Preheat moulds to drive off moisture

### ☠️ Preventing poisoning
- Always work outdoors or somewhere well ventilated
- Avoid breathing metal fumes
- Heavy metals such as lead and mercury are extremely toxic; handle with great caution
- Wash your hands after work

### 🩹 Treating burns
- Rinse at once with cool water for 15-20 minutes (do not use ice)
- Do not burst blisters
- Do not apply grease, soy sauce, toothpaste or the like
- Cover with a clean, damp gauze; for severe burns, get medical help at once
- For details see [Wound and injury care – Scalds and burns](../../../docs/01-日常疾病和伤害/02-外伤处理.md#烫伤与烧伤)

## 💡 Advice for beginners

### ✅ Recommended start
1. **Start with copper**: low melting point, easy to work, high success rate
2. **Collect scrap metal**: easier than smelting ore, and good for practising forging, melting and casting (see [Scrap metal recovery and aluminium casting](../../../docs/05-冶金技术/05-废旧金属回收与铝铸造.md))
3. **Start with simple tools**: nails, blades and the like
4. **Build a team**: metallurgy is heavy labour and needs a division of work

### ❌ Common mistakes
- ✗ Trying to make liquid iron the first time (too hard, and you will be tempted to give up; beginners should start with the bloomery process)
- ✗ Not enough air into the furnace (the temperature will not rise)
- ✗ Overheating the work before quenching (it cracks)
- ✗ Working alone (dangerous and inefficient)

### 💰 Resource priorities
1. **Charcoal** - the most important; used in huge amounts
2. **Clay** - essential for building furnaces
3. **Ore** - choose copper or iron according to difficulty
4. **Water** - for cooling and safety

## 🌍 Alternatives

When smelting is completely impossible:

### Salvage and reuse
See [Scrap metal recovery and aluminium casting](../../../docs/05-冶金技术/05-废旧金属回收与铝铸造.md).
- Collect discarded metal objects and remelt them
- Old car parts (lots of steel)
- Reinforcing bars from abandoned buildings
- Copper wire from old appliances

### Other materials
- **Stone tools**: a short-term substitute
- **Bone tools**: needles, fish hooks and so on
- **Bamboo and wooden tools**: temporary tools
- **Pottery**: substitute containers

## 📖 Glossary

- **Pig iron**: 2-4% carbon; hard but brittle; cannot be forged
- **Wrought iron**: <0.2% carbon; soft but tough; easy to forge
- **Steel**: 0.2-2% carbon; both hard and tough
- **Reduction**: removing oxygen from an oxide to obtain the metal
- **Slag**: the waste produced by smelting
- **Quenching**: rapid cooling from high temperature, which increases hardness
- **Tempering**: reheating after quenching, which reduces brittleness
- **Carburising**: increasing the carbon content at the surface
- **Forging**: heating and hammering into shape
- **Casting**: melting and pouring into a mould
