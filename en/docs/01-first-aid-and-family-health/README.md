# First Aid and Family Health Handbook

## 📋 Overview

This part covers first aid, dealing with common illnesses and injuries, and long-term health care for family members. It is in three groups: **first aid**, which you need the moment something happens; **common illnesses and long-term health**, which you look after day to day; and **special groups** such as pregnant women and new mothers, older people, children and women.

## ⚠️ Important reminders

### When you must get medical help immediately

In any of these situations, call the emergency number at once or go to hospital:

- ❌ Unconsciousness or confusion
- ❌ Difficulty breathing, or breathing has stopped
- ❌ Chest pain or an abnormal heartbeat (see [Heart attack and chest pain](06-heart-stroke-and-sudden-illness.md#heart-attack-and-chest-pain))
- ❌ Sudden drooping of the mouth, weakness on one side of the body, slurred speech (stroke; see [Stroke](06-heart-stroke-and-sudden-illness.md#stroke))
- ❌ Several people have a headache and feel dizzy at the same time — suspect carbon monoxide poisoning
- ❌ A pregnant woman has vaginal bleeding, a severe headache with blurred vision, convulsions, fewer baby movements, or is about to give birth (see [Emergency childbirth](11-emergency-childbirth-and-newborn-care.md))
- ❌ Someone says they want to kill themselves or is preparing to harm themselves (see [Mental health – suicide risk](10-mental-health.md#suicide-risk-recognition-and-response))
- ❌ Severe bleeding that cannot be stopped
- ❌ A serious blow to the head
- ❌ A fracture with obvious deformity
- ❌ Severe burns (large in area or deep)
- ❌ Poisoning or swallowing medicines by mistake (see [Poisoning](12-poisoning.md); do not make them vomit; take the medicine bottle or pesticide bottle with you)
- ❌ A severe allergic reaction (difficulty breathing, swelling all over)
- ❌ Body temperature ≥ 40°C
- ❌ Any fever in a baby under 3 months old
- ❌ Fever with a stiff neck, convulsions, a change in consciousness or a rash that does not fade when pressed
  (for fever grades and detailed criteria for seeing a doctor, see [Common illnesses – fever](01-common-illnesses.md#fever))

### Basic principles of first aid

1. **Protect yourself** - you can only help others if you are safe
2. **Assess the scene** - judge whether the surroundings are safe
3. **Call for help** - call the emergency number promptly
4. **Give first aid** - help as far as your abilities allow
5. **Keep watching** - monitor how the casualty's condition changes

## 🏥 What every first aid kit needs

### Basic first aid kit

- Sterile gauze (several sizes)
- Medical tape
- Adhesive plasters (several sizes)
- Bandages (elastic and triangular)
- Medical gloves
- Scissors and tweezers
- Safety pins
- Thermometer
- Disposable masks

### Common medicines

- Povidone-iodine or alcohol disinfectant
- Antibacterial ointment
- Petroleum jelly or aloe vera gel (for minor scalds; do not keep medicated burn ointments to use on your own, see [Wounds and injuries](02-wounds-and-injuries.md#scalds-and-burns))
- Painkillers (paracetamol (acetaminophen), ibuprofen)
- Allergy medicine (antihistamines)
- Anti-diarrhoeal medicine
- Oral rehydration salts (ORS)
- Eye drops

### Extra tools

- Torch
- Whistle
- First aid manual
- List of emergency contacts
- Prescriptions for personal medicines

## 📞 Emergency phone numbers

**Mainland China**
- Ambulance: 120
- Poisoning: 120 (mainland China has no national poison information hotline yet)
- Children's emergencies: 120
- Text-message police report: 12110 (use when you cannot make a call; add the province code after the number)
- Mental health helpline: 12356 (nationwide, 24 hours)

**Remember**: when you call the emergency number, state clearly:
1. Location and address
2. The casualty's condition
3. What has been done so far
4. How to contact you
