## How infectious diseases spread

| Route | Common diseases | Main protection |
|------|----------|----------|
| Droplets, air | Flu, COVID-19, tuberculosis, measles, chickenpox | Wear a mask, ventilate, keep your distance |
| Contact (hands, objects) | Norovirus, hand, foot and mouth disease, pink eye (conjunctivitis) | Wash hands, disinfect frequently touched surfaces |
| Faecal-oral (dirty water, dirty hands, flies) | Cholera, dysentery, typhoid, hepatitis A, norovirus | Wash hands, drink boiled water, eat cooked food, manage faeces safely |
| Blood, body fluids | Hepatitis B, HIV/AIDS | Do not share razors or toothbrushes; wear gloves when dealing with wounds |
| Mosquitoes and insects | Malaria, dengue, Japanese encephalitis | Prevent mosquito bites, get rid of standing water |

**Handwashing** is the simplest and most effective way to stop most infectious diseases; for how, see [Sanitation - handwashing stations](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/03-卫生设施.md#洗手点).

## When someone at home has an infectious disease

### Isolating at home

```
The sick person:
✓ If possible, stays in a separate room with the door closed and the window open
✓ If there is no separate room: keep more than 1 m apart, hang a curtain between, sleep in separate beds
✓ Wears a mask when in contact with family members
✓ Uses their own towel, bowls, chopsticks and cup
✓ Uses a separate toilet if possible; if shared, disinfect it after use

The carer:
✓ If possible, the same one person does all the caring (preferably not an older person, a pregnant woman or someone with a chronic illness)
✓ Wear a mask when entering the sick person's room; wear gloves when touching their secretions, vomit or faeces
✓ Wash hands straight after leaving the room and after taking off gloves
✓ If you develop a fever or other symptoms yourself, see a doctor promptly and tell them about your contact

The home:
✓ Open the windows to air the home several times a day (at least 30 minutes each time)
✓ Disinfect frequently touched spots every day: door handles, switches, taps, toilet flush buttons, tabletops, phones
✓ Wash the sick person's clothes and bedding separately, in hot water if the fabric allows
✓ Put used tissues and masks in a bag, tie it tightly and throw it away
```

### When to see a doctor

```
- High fever that will not come down, very low energy
- Rapid breathing, tight chest, bluish lips
- Severe vomiting and diarrhoea, signs of dehydration (passing little urine, dry mouth, sunken eyes)
- Changes in consciousness, fits, stiff neck
- Rash with fever (especially pinpoint bleeding spots that do not fade when pressed)
- Babies and young children, older people, pregnant women or people with chronic illness getting worse
```

For fever grades and when to seek medical care, see [Common illnesses - fever](01-common-illnesses.md#fever).

⚠️ Once a notifiable infectious disease of Class A, Class B, etc. (such as cholera, tuberculosis or measles) is confirmed, follow the isolation and treatment requirements of the medical institution and the disease control authorities.

## Making household disinfectant

### "84" bleach (chlorine-based disinfectant)

The ratios below are worked out for "84" bleach with **about 5% available chlorine**. Concentration differs between brands; **first check the available chlorine content printed on the bottle**, and adjust the ratio in proportion if it differs.

| Use | Available chlorine concentration | "84" bleach : water | Example |
|------|-----------|---------------|------|
| Everyday surfaces, tableware, clothing | about 500 mg/L | 1 : 100 | 10 mL undiluted bleach + 1 L water |
| Items used by the sick person, contaminated floors, toilets | about 1000 mg/L | 1 : 50 | 20 mL undiluted bleach + 1 L water |
| Vomit, faeces and similar contamination | 5000-10000 mg/L | about 1 : 9 to 1 : 4 | about 110-250 mL undiluted bleach + 1 L water |

```
How to use:
✓ Dilute with cold or room-temperature water (hot water breaks down the active ingredient and releases more irritating gas)
✓ Mix fresh each time; use up diluted solution the same day
✓ After wiping or soaking, leave for about 30 minutes, then wipe or rinse off with clean water
  (rinse metal items, tableware and clothing thoroughly with clean water after disinfecting)
✓ Wear gloves and open the windows

Dealing with vomit and faeces:
1. Wear gloves and a mask
2. Soak tissues, rags or other disposable absorbent material in strong disinfectant and cover the mess completely
3. Leave for 30 minutes, then carefully remove it, bag it, tie the bag tightly and throw it away
4. Then wipe and disinfect the contaminated floor and the area around it with 1:50 disinfectant
5. Take off gloves and wash hands

⚠️ Safety:
❌ Never mix with acidic cleaners such as toilet cleaner (this produces chlorine gas; see Poisoning - cleaning product poisoning)
❌ Do not use on skin or wounds
❌ Do not keep diluted solution in drinks bottles; label it and keep it out of children's reach
⚠️ It corrodes metal and bleaches clothing (dark clothes will fade)
```

### Other ways to disinfect

| Method | How to use | Notes |
|------|------|------|
| Boiling | Heat-resistant items such as tableware, towels and baby bottles: boil for at least 15 minutes after the water comes to the boil | Simple and reliable; first choice when power or supplies are cut |
| 75% alcohol | Wipe hands, small items, phones | Flammable; do not spray over large areas; **works poorly against norovirus** |
| Sunlight | Expose bedding and clothes to strong sunlight for several hours | Only a supplement; cannot replace disinfection |

For making alcohol yourself and its strength, see [Disinfectant preparation](../../../docs/06-制药技术/01-消毒剂制备.md).

## Diarrhoeal infections

Norovirus, cholera, dysentery, typhoid, hepatitis A and similar diseases spread by the faecal-oral route. They break out especially easily when the water supply is cut, after floods, and in crowded relief camps.

```
Prevention:
✓ Drink only boiled water or safe bottled water
✓ Cook food thoroughly, keep raw and cooked food apart, reheat leftovers until piping hot
✓ Wash hands before eating and after using the toilet (with soap and running water)
✓ Manage faeces safely; keep toilets away from water sources (see Sanitation)
✓ Kill flies
✓ Sick people must not cook for others

When someone has diarrhoea or is vomiting:
✓ Replace fluids promptly; oral rehydration salts (ORS) are the first choice (see Common illnesses - diarrhoea)
✓ Deal with vomit and faeces using strong disinfectant as described above
✓ Boil the sick person's tableware to disinfect it
✓ The person stays infectious for at least 2-3 days after symptoms stop; keep washing hands and do not cook

⚠️ Large amounts of watery diarrhoea (like rice-washing water) with rapid dehydration in a short time → may be cholera; see a doctor at once and report it
⚠️ Several people in the same household or group with diarrhoea at the same time → report to the local disease control centre or township health centre
```

Related chapters: [Sanitation](../../../docs/07-日常生活技能/03-卫生设施.md), [Common illnesses - diarrhoea](01-common-illnesses.md#diarrhoea).

## Respiratory infections

```
✓ Cover coughs and sneezes with a tissue or your elbow; put the tissue in the bin and wash your hands
✓ Wear a mask when you have symptoms; avoid crowded places
✓ Open the windows indoors often
✓ In flu season, older people, children and people with chronic illness should avoid crowded, enclosed places where possible

⚠️ Tuberculosis:
- Cough or coughing up phlegm for more than 2 weeks, or blood in the phlegm, with low fever, night sweats and weight loss
  → get checked as soon as possible at a hospital or a tuberculosis control institution
- Tuberculosis can be cured, but the whole course of treatment must be completed as prescribed (usually six months or more);
  stopping partway easily leads to drug resistance
- China has a policy of free testing and treatment for tuberculosis patients; ask the local tuberculosis control institution
```

## Vaccines

```
✓ Children complete the National Immunisation Programme vaccines on schedule (free)
✓ Older people and people with chronic illness get a flu vaccine every year (if available)
✓ After an animal bite, get rabies vaccine (see Wounds and injuries - bites)
✓ After an injury, get tetanus vaccine as needed (see Wounds and injuries - tetanus prevention)
✓ Keep vaccination records safe (take them with you if you have to move after a disaster)
```

Related chapters: [Wounds and injuries - bites](02-wounds-and-injuries.md#bites), [Wounds and injuries - tetanus prevention](02-wounds-and-injuries.md#tetanus-prevention).

## 📝 Infectious disease at home: key points

```
1. Frequent handwashing is the most effective measure
2. The sick person has a separate room, wears a mask, has their own tableware and towels; one person does the caring
3. Air the home every day; disinfect frequently touched spots
4. "84" bleach (5% available chlorine): everyday 1:100, contaminated 1:50, vomit and faeces about 1:9 to 1:4
5. Mix "84" bleach with cold water, mix fresh, never mix with toilet cleaner
6. Alcohol works poorly against norovirus; boiling is the most reliable way to disinfect
7. Drink boiled water, eat cooked food, manage faeces safely, kill flies
8. Cough for more than 2 weeks: get checked for tuberculosis
```
