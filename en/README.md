# Human Emergency Survival and Health Guide

[![License: MIT](https://img.shields.io/badge/License-MIT-yellow.svg)](https://opensource.org/licenses/MIT)

## 📖 About

This is a comprehensive emergency survival and health handbook. It aims to give people practical guidance and help when they face emergencies, illness and injury, natural disasters, and the challenges of survival in the wild.

**⚠️ Important notice**: The information in this handbook is for reference and education only. In a real emergency, always seek help from medical professionals first. This handbook is not a substitute for professional medical advice, diagnosis or treatment.

> **About this English edition**: This is a translation of a handbook written for mainland China ([Chinese original](README.md)). Emergency numbers (120 ambulance, 110 police, 119 fire), laws and regulations, which medicines are available, and where plants and animals are found all reflect China — substitute your local equivalents. Parts not yet translated link to the Chinese original.

## 📚 Contents

### [I. First Aid and Family Health Handbook](docs/01-first-aid-and-family-health/README.md)

First aid, dealing with common illnesses and injuries, and long-term health care for family members, in three groups: first aid, common illnesses and long-term health, and special groups.

**First aid**
- **Basic first aid**: assessing the scene and calling 120, vital signs, airway and the recovery position, CPR and AED, stopping bleeding, shock, moving casualties
- **Wounds and injuries**: cuts, grazes, scalds, burns, frostbite, bites, etc.
- **Bone and joint injuries**: fractures, dislocations, sprains, etc.
- **Foreign bodies**: objects in the eye, ear, nose or throat
- **Acute conditions**: allergic reactions, heatstroke, hypothermia, electric shock, drowning
- **Heart, stroke and sudden illness**: stroke, heart attack, low blood sugar, epileptic seizures, asthma attacks
- **Home and traffic accidents**: carbon monoxide poisoning, at the scene of a road accident
- **Poisoning**: pesticides and rat poison, drug overdose and accidental swallowing, alcohol poisoning, mixing cleaning products

**Common illnesses and long-term health**
- **Common illnesses**: colds, fever and fever-medicine doses, diarrhoea and rehydration, vomiting, food poisoning, constipation, headache, cough
- **Stockpiling medicines for chronic illness**: the risks of stopping medicines suddenly, long-term prescriptions, storing insulin, medication information cards
- **Dental problems**: toothache, danger signs of a dental abscess, knocked-out teeth, bleeding after an extraction
- **Mental health**: normal reactions after a crisis, psychological first aid, suicide risk, grief, children's mental health
- **Infectious disease at home**: home isolation, making up "84" bleach solution, diarrhoeal and respiratory infections, vaccines
- **Skin conditions and parasites**: scabies, head lice, bedbugs, ringworm, impetigo

**Special groups**
- **Emergency childbirth and newborn care**: danger signs in pregnancy, delivering a baby when there is no time to reach hospital, the newborn's golden minute, postpartum haemorrhage
- **Elder care**: preventing falls, what to do after a fall, care for the bedridden, dementia and wandering
- **Child safety**: preventing drowning, falls, accidental swallowing, choking and scalds; car safety; preventing children getting lost
- **Menstrual hygiene and contraception**: menstrual products, home-made cloth pads, contraception, emergency contraception

### [II. Disaster Response Handbook](../docs/02-灾难应对/README.md)

Prevention, response and self-rescue for all kinds of natural and man-made disasters.

- **Geological disasters**: earthquakes (preparation, protecting yourself, being trapped and rescuing others), debris flows, landslides, ground collapse, volcanic eruptions
- **Weather disasters**: warning signals, typhoons, floods and urban flooding, blizzards, tornadoes, hail, heat waves, drought, sandstorms, lightning
- **Ocean disasters**: tsunamis, storm surges, rip currents, falling overboard, red tides and toxic seafood, safety at the seaside
- **Fire**: fighting a fire in its early stage and fire extinguishers, escaping building fires, forest fires, electrical fires, gas leaks
- **Man-made disasters**: war and unrest, chemical spills, nuclear and radiation accidents, terrorist attacks, crowd crushes, power and water cuts
- **Emergency communication and finding family**: family communication plans, text messages first, saving phone battery, walkie-talkies, finding each other after separation
- **Handling the dead after disasters**: myths about bodies and epidemics, protection, identification and records, temporary storage and burial

### [III. Wilderness Survival Guide](../docs/03-野外生存/README.md)

From outdoor safety on a weekend hike to extreme environments and long-term survival.

**Basics**
- **Basic survival skills**: survival priorities, navigation, finding and purifying water, making fire, shelter, distress signals

**Outdoor safety** (read these first if you hike, climb or camp)
- **Trip preparation and calling for help**: trip plans, day-hike kit, getting lost and reporting your location, contour lines
- **River crossings, flash floods and hypothermia in the wild**: signs of a flash flood, choosing a campsite, crossing rivers, falling into water, hypothermia outside winter
- **Poisonous mushrooms and wild plants**: folk identification tests are unreliable, the false-recovery period, poisonous plants that are easily mistaken
- **Wasps, ticks and dangerous animals**: wasp stings, removing ticks, land leeches, snakes, wild boar
- **Treating and evacuating the sick and injured in the wild**: stay put or walk out, recording injuries, improvised splints, sprained ankles and blisters, carrying casualties

**Extreme environments**
- **Desert island survival**: getting water, gathering food, building shelter, distress signals
- **Forest survival**: finding direction, wild food, recognising dangerous plants and animals
- **Desert survival**: finding water, protection from sun and heat, desert navigation
- **Snow and polar survival**: keeping warm, preventing frostbite, snow shelters, avalanches, ice
- **Mountain survival**: dealing with altitude sickness, rock shelters, mountain navigation, falls and rockfall

**Long-term survival**
- **Survival after civilisation collapse**: long-term survival strategy, stockpiling resources, building community

### [IV. Growing Plants and Raising Animals](../docs/04-动植物培育/README.md)

How to grow and raise common edible and useful plants and animals.

- **Vegetable growing**: sowing times and germination temperatures, crop rotation and replant problems, hotbeds and low tunnels, compost, raising and transplanting seedlings, pests and diseases, individual vegetables, the winter vegetable gap
- **Staple crops**: rice, wheat, maize, potatoes, sweet potatoes (the highest-yielding), soya beans, sorghum
- **Fruit trees**: choosing varieties and rootstocks, planting out, training and pruning, fertilising, pollination, thinning flowers and fruit, frost protection, pests and diseases, years to bearing and yields
- **Medicinal plants**: identification and poisonous look-alikes, growing, harvesting and drying, normal use and contraindications, warnings about using them alongside Western medicines
- **Poultry**: keeping chickens, ducks and geese; brooding chicks, home-mixed feed, incubation, disease prevention and zoonoses, hygienic slaughter
- **Livestock**: keeping and breeding pigs, sheep, goats and rabbits; key points for draught animals such as cattle and donkeys; disease prevention, zoonoses and hygienic slaughter
- **Fish farming**: ponds and pond cleaning, polyculture stocking densities, water quality and emergency action when fish gasp at the surface, feeding, fish and ducks in rice paddies, fish diseases, harvesting and keeping fish fresh, safety around ponds
- **Seed saving and breeding**: self- and cross-pollination, isolation distances and hand pollination, saving seed from the main crops, drying and storing seed, germination tests, cuttings and grafting, selection year by year, community seed banks
- **Firewood and woodland management**: how much wood a household and a kiln burn, heat value and drying, woodland yield, coppice rotation, tree planting, protecting forests, other fuels, saving fuel, felling safety

### [V. Metallurgy](../docs/05-冶金技术/README.md)

Smelting metal and making tools from scratch after a collapse.

> ⚠️ **Only for extreme situations such as the collapse of civilisation and of the industrial system.** High-temperature work, toxic fumes, and molten metal exploding on contact with water: do not attempt this outside an emergency.

- **Basic metallurgy**: building furnaces, choosing fuel, controlling temperature
- **Iron smelting**: identifying iron ore, smelting iron, turning pig iron into steel
- **Copper smelting**: processing copper ore, making bronze
- **Simple tool forging**: making knives, axes, hoes, sickles and nails
- **Metalworking techniques**: quenching, tempering, annealing, normalising, carburising
- **Scrap metal recovery and aluminium casting**: identifying metals, good steel in the ruins, safe dismantling, melting and casting aluminium
- **Welding and joining**: riveting, forge welding, soldering, brazing, arc welding
- **Advanced casting**: crucible furnaces, moulding sand and moulding, gating systems, cast iron, casting defects
- **Blowing equipment and forges**: skin bellows, double-acting piston bellows, water-powered bellows, the forge and managing the fire
- **Fitting and machining**: sawing, filing and drilling, tapping and threading, scraping, the lathe, tolerances and interchangeability
- **Other metals**: sources of tin, lead, zinc, gold and silver, the principles of smelting them, and protection
- **Sheet and wire**: annealing, rolling and spreading, tinplate, wire drawing and electrical wire

### [VI. Pharmaceuticals](../docs/06-制药技术/README.md)

What to do when civilisation has collapsed and commercial medicines cannot be obtained. **The focus is on stockpiling in advance and workable alternatives, not on making medicines yourself.**

> ⚠️ **Never make your own medicines when modern medical care is available.** This part also explains which medicines really cannot be made at home, so that you are not misled by folk remedies.

- **Basic pharmaceutical knowledge**: safety rules, dose conversion and weighing errors, storage and shelf life, expired medicines, side effects and when to see a doctor
- **Making disinfectants**: distilling alcohol, making tincture of iodine, the concentrations of each disinfectant and what it is suitable for, combinations that must not be mixed (the most feasible and most useful category)
- **Antibiotics**: why you cannot make them yourself, how to stockpile them, natural antibacterial substances (honey, etc.)
- **Fever and pain medicines**: willow bark decoction; why aspirin and paracetamol cannot be made at home; principles, contraindications and when-to-see-a-doctor criteria for using ready-made medicines
- **Traditional Chinese medicine preparations**: processing and decocting common Chinese herbs (doses according to the Chinese Pharmacopoeia), people who must not take them, toxic Chinese herbs, warnings about using them alongside Western medicines

### [VII. Everyday Living Skills](../docs/07-日常生活技能/README.md)

The most basic skills for long-term living, and the easiest to overlook.

- **Food processing and preservation**: sun-drying, salting, fermenting, smoking, cellar storage, canning; high-risk foods (bongkrekic acid, botulism, aflatoxin)
- **Nutrition and deficiency diseases**: recognising and preventing scurvy, beriberi, pellagra and others
- **Sanitation**: emergency toilets, dry toilets, handwashing stations, using excreta, waste disposal
- **Soap making**: soap pods and soapnuts, making soap with plant-ash lye
- **Long-term housing**: choosing a site, keeping out damp, adobe and rammed-earth walls, timber frames, earthquake resistance, insulation, the *kang* (heated brick bed), stoves and chimneys
- **Lighting and energy**: emergency lighting, home-made oil lamps and candles, fuel storage, generators, small-scale solar power, biogas
- **Textiles, mending and tanning**: mending stitches, spinning, knitting and weaving, tanning hides and furs
- **Household electrical safety**: residual-current devices, power strips, charging electric bikes, restoring power after a disaster
- **Woodworking and tool maintenance**: drying timber, basic joints, replacing tool handles, sharpening, tool care
- **Water supply engineering**: protecting water sources, digging wells safely, collecting rainwater, slow sand filtration, disinfectant doses, bringing and lifting water
- **Pottery, glass and papermaking**: choosing and preparing clay, shaping and drying, pit firing and updraught kilns, glazes and waterproofing, bricks and tiles, recycling and remelting glass and annealing, papermaking, carbon-black ink
- **Teaching and knowledge preservation**: what to teach first, literacy and arithmetic, apprenticeship and teaching crafts, running a small school, writing operating manuals, copying, proofreading and preserving records
- **Record-keeping, trade and division of labour**: day books and stocktaking, IOUs, stock and days of supply left, rationing, standard measures and barter, work points, skills registers and division of labour, preventing errors and fraud, community archives
- **Grain and oil processing**: harvesting, threshing, winnowing, drying to a safe moisture content, storing grain against mould and insects, hulling and milling rice, grinding flour with a millstone, treating maize with limewater, pressing oil and storing it, workshop safety
- **Salt making**: how much salt you need, "salts" that must not be eaten, brine concentration, solar salt pans, leaching and boiling brine, salt from salt lakes, springs, wells and salty earth, purification and bittern, iodine, storage
- **Lime and building materials**: the lime cycle, lime kilns, slaking and ageing, lime mortar and *sanhetu* (lime-clay-sand concrete), whitewashing and disinfection, improving acid soil, gypsum, recovering cement and recycled aggregate
- **Rope and knots**: fibre sources, twisting and making rope, rope strength and safety factors, a dozen or so common knots, splicing, lashing, building and hoisting, care
- **Vehicles, boats and roads**: bicycles and handcarts, carrying loads by hand, wheels and axles, draught animals and harness, road drainage and surfacing, bridges, rafts and boats, crossing rivers, comparing ways of transport

### [VIII. Basic Science](../docs/08-基础科学知识/README.md)

The mathematics, physics, chemistry, biology and medicine that have most shaped human society, as the theoretical foundation for rebuilding technology from scratch.

- **Basics of biology and medicine**: germ theory and how disinfection works, immunity and vaccines, antibiotics and resistance, deficiency diseases, heredity and selection, epidemiology
- **Periodic table**: the full periodic table, data for 118 elements, periodic trends and valence, how Chinese element names are formed
- **Geology, minerals and soil**: identifying rocks and minerals, finding ore, clay and groundwater, soil texture, pH and fertility
- **Weather observation and forecasting**: reading clouds, air pressure and wind, the Beaufort scale, frost, home-made instruments and a weather diary
- **Surveying and mapping**: survey ropes, ranging poles and home-made angle-measuring instruments, contour lines with an A-frame, triangulation, levelling and setting canal gradients, traverse surveys and drawing maps, coordinates, calculating areas and recording boundaries
- **Rebuilding measurement standards**: calibrating the second by the stars, fixing the metre from a seconds pendulum and the size of the Earth, fixing the kilogram with water, fixed points of temperature, ready-made references in the ruins, how big each unit really is, calibrating electrical units, preserving the standards, units in old books

### [X. Identifying and Collecting Chinese Medicinal Herbs](../docs/10-中药材识别与采集/README.md)

A field guide to common Chinese medicinal herbs: how to recognise them, where to find them, when to collect them and how to process and store them, with Pharmacopoeia doses, contraindications and poisonous plants that are easily confused with them.

- **General principles**: classification, botanical source and identification by appearance, collection times, processing at the place of origin, storage, dosage and safety, laws protecting wild medicinal plants

- **Flowers and leaves, barks, stems and woods, fungi, algae, resins and others**: 67 herbs, including *Jinyinhua* (honeysuckle flower), *Xihonghua* (saffron), *Aiye* (mugwort leaf), *Fanxieye* (senna leaf), *Duzhong* (eucommia bark), *Huangbai* (phellodendron bark), *Rendongteng* (honeysuckle stem), *Chenxiang* (agarwood), *Fuling* (poria), *Lingzhi* (reishi), *Dongchong Xiacao* (cordyceps, a protected species) and *Bingpian* (borneol)

- **Toxic Chinese herbs and poisonous look-alikes**: the 28 toxic Chinese medicines, highly toxic herbs, herbs containing aristolochic acid, telling apart easily confused poisonous plants such as *Gouwen* (Gelsemium elegans, heartbreak grass)

## 🎯 How to use this handbook

1. **Emergencies**: quickly find the relevant chapter and follow the emergency steps
2. **Preparation**: read the relevant chapters systematically and learn the basic skills
3. **Regular review**: go back over the important content regularly to keep your skills sharp
4. **Practice**: practise the skills in a safe setting
5. **Units**: values use the international symbols of SI units throughout; see [Unit symbols](#-unit-symbols) below for what they mean

## 📏 Unit symbols

Values in this handbook use the international symbols of the International System of Units (SI), with a space between the number and the symbol (e.g. `1.5 m`, `500 g`). Times in running text are written in words (e.g. "rinse for 15 minutes", "wait 3 days"); times in formulas, data tables and compound units use symbols (e.g. m/s, kWh). Traditional Chinese and imperial units have been converted to SI units; for the conversions see [Measurement and scientific method – common unit conversions](../docs/08-基础科学知识/00-度量衡与科学方法.md#三常用单位换算).

## 📖 Recommended reading order

### Beginners
1. First read [Basic first aid](docs/01-first-aid-and-family-health/00-basic-first-aid.md)
2. Get to know [Common illnesses](docs/01-first-aid-and-family-health/01-common-illnesses.md)
3. Learn [Wounds and injuries](docs/01-first-aid-and-family-health/02-wounds-and-injuries.md)

### Further study
1. Master the basic principles of [Disaster response](../docs/02-灾难应对/README.md)
2. Learn the basic skills of [Wilderness survival](../docs/03-野外生存/README.md)
3. Get to know the basics of [Growing plants and raising animals](../docs/04-动植物培育/README.md)
4. Learn [Everyday living skills](../docs/07-日常生活技能/README.md): food preservation, nutrition, sanitation

### Preparing for collapse
1. **Stockpile in advance**: medicines, wound-care supplies, seeds, tools (see [Pharmaceuticals – stockpiling advice](../docs/06-制药技术/02-抗生素制备.md#六储备建议))
2. Learn [Metallurgy](../docs/05-冶金技术/README.md) to make basic tools
3. Learn the feasible parts of [Pharmaceuticals](../docs/06-制药技术/README.md) (disinfectants, Chinese herbal medicine, natural antibacterial substances)
4. Learn [Basic science](../docs/08-基础科学知识/README.md): measurement, mathematics, physics, chemistry, and the principles of biology and medicine, so that you can work things out for yourself when a situation is not covered in the book
5. Read the [Technology rebuilding roadmap](../docs/09-进阶科学与工程/00-技术重建路线图.md) to understand the overall order of rebuilding technology and which things to preserve first
6. Build up a body of knowledge for long-term survival

## 🤝 Contributing

We welcome contributions of every kind! If you have:

- professional medical knowledge
- experience of wilderness survival
- experience of dealing with disasters
- experience of farming
- found a mistake in the documents or something that could be improved

please take part as follows:

1. Fork this project
2. Create your feature branch (`git checkout -b feature/AmazingFeature`)
3. Commit your changes (`git commit -m '添加某某内容'`)
4. Push to the branch (`git push origin feature/AmazingFeature`)
5. Open a pull request

Before committing, run `python3 tools/check_docs.py` to check for broken links, anchors, code blocks and how units are written (this also runs automatically on pull requests). After changing herb names or alternative names in part X, run `python3 tools/build_herb_index.py` to regenerate the [herb name index](../docs/10-中药材识别与采集/10-药名索引.md). After adding or removing diagrams, run `python3 tools/build_figure_index.py` to regenerate the [diagram index](#-diagram-index) above.

English chapters live in `en/` and must keep exactly the same heading structure as the Chinese original (the same number of headings, at the same levels, in the same order); `tools/check_docs.py` checks this. `en/paths.tsv` maps each Chinese file to its English file, and `tools/en_links.py` maintains the cross-links: once a chapter has been translated, it rewrites links that still point to the Chinese original so they point to the English version.
